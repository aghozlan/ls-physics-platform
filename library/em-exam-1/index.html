Energy & Momentum — Practice Exam 1

Three exercises covering Energy and Linear Momentum, medium level, official-exam format. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This exam is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — The spring launcher7 pts

A toy launcher is formed of a spring of stiffness k=200 N/m, placed on a horizontal track ABC and fixed from one end. A block (S), considered as a particle of mass m=0.5 kg, is placed against its free end. The spring is compressed by x0​=AO=10 cm, O being the position of the free end of the unstretched spring (Doc. 1).

The horizontal part AB is frictionless; BC is a straight incline making an angle α=30∘ with the horizontal. (S) leaves the spring at O.
The horizontal plane through AB is taken as the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — The launch

  1. 1.1(S) is released without initial velocity from A. Calculate the mechanical energy of the system [(S), spring, Earth] at the instant of release.
  2. 1.2Justify that this mechanical energy is conserved between A and O. Deduce that (S) leaves the spring at O with a speed V0​=2 m/s.
Part 1 · 1.75 pts

Part 2 — Climbing the incline without friction

In this part, friction on BC is neglected. A point M of BC is located by its abscissa x=BM along the incline; the speed of (S) at M is v. (S) reaches B with the speed V0​.

  1. 2.1Write the expression of the mechanical energy of the system [(S), Earth] at M in terms of m, v, g, x and α.
  2. 2.2Deduce the maximum distance L covered by (S) on BC.
  3. 2.3By deriving the mechanical energy with respect to time, show that the acceleration of (S) is a=−gsinα. Deduce the nature of the motion of (S) on BC.
Part 2 · 3 pts

Part 3 — The real incline

In reality, (S) is acted upon on BC by a constant friction force f​ parallel to the incline. (S) stops at a point D such that BD=25 cm.

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation ΔME of the mechanical energy of the system [(S), Earth] between B and D.
  2. 3.2Deduce the magnitude f of the friction force and the variation ΔU of the internal energy of the system [(S), incline, Earth].
Part 3 · 2.25 pts

Exercise 2 — Two skaters push each other7 pts

Two ice-skaters, (A) of mass mA​=60 kg and (B) of mass mB​=40 kg, are standing at rest face to face on a horizontal ice rink (Doc. 2). They push each other with their hands. Friction on the ice is neglected.

Part 1 — The system [(A), (B)]

  1. 1.1Name the external forces acting on the system [(A), (B)]. Show that this system is pseudo-isolated.
  2. 1.2Give the value of the linear momentum P of the system before the push. Deduce its value P′ just after the push.
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — Just after the push

Just after the push, (A) moves with the velocity vA​=−2i (m/s).

  1. 2.1Determine the velocity vB​ of (B) just after the push.
  2. 2.2Calculate the kinetic energy of the system [(A), (B)] just after the push. Specify the origin of this energy.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — (B) on a rough zone

(B) then enters a rough zone of the rink at the instant t0​=0, where it is acted upon by a constant horizontal friction force f​. Doc. 3 shows the magnitude P of the linear momentum of (B) as a function of time.

  1. 3.1Using Doc. 3 and Newton's second law ΣF=dtdP​, determine the magnitude f of the friction force.
  2. 3.2By applying the variation of the mechanical energy of the system [(B), Earth], determine the distance d covered by (B) on the rough zone until it stops.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts

Exercise 3 — Two carts and a bumper6 pts

A cart (A) of mass mA​=1 kg is released without initial velocity from a point M of a ramp, at a height h=45 cm above the horizontal track (Doc. 4). A cart (B) of mass mB​=2 kg is at rest on the track. A bumper, a spring of stiffness k=300 N/m, is fixed at the end of the track. Friction is neglected everywhere; the carts are considered as particles.

The horizontal plane of the track is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — Down the ramp

  1. 1.1Justify that the mechanical energy of the system [(A), Earth] is conserved. Calculate the speed vA​ of (A) at the bottom N of the ramp.
Part 1 · 1.25 pts

Part 2 — The collision

(A) hits (B); the two carts stick together and move with a common velocity V=Vi.

  1. 2.1The system [(A), (B)] is pseudo-isolated during the collision. Determine V.
  2. 2.2Calculate the kinetic energy of the system [(A), (B)] before and after the collision. Deduce the type of this collision.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — The bumper

  1. 3.1The two carts compress the bumper. Calculate the maximum compression X of the spring.
  2. 3.2The carts leave the bumper with the velocity V′=−Vi. Calculate the variation ΔP of the linear momentum of the carts. Deduce the average force exerted by the bumper on the carts, knowing that the contact lasts Δt=0.2 s.
Part 3 · 2.25 pts
