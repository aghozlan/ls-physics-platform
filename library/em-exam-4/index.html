Energy & Momentum — Practice Exam 4

Three exercises on Energy and Linear Momentum, medium level, official-exam format, with a focus on perfectly elastic collisions. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This exam is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — Finding an unknown mass7 pts

On a horizontal table, a ball (A) of mass mA​=200 g, moving with the velocity vA​=3i (m/s), collides head-on with a ball (B) of unknown mass mB​, initially at rest (Doc. 1). The collision is perfectly elastic. Just after the collision, (A) rebounds with the velocity vA′​=−1i (m/s) and (B) moves with the velocity vB′​=vB′​i. Friction is neglected; the balls are considered as particles.

Part 1 — Before the collision

  1. 1.1Show that the system [(A), (B)] is pseudo-isolated.
  2. 1.2Calculate the linear momentum of the system before the collision.
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — The two unknowns

  1. 2.1Write the two equations that express the conservations during this collision, in terms of mA​, mB​, vA​, vA′​ and vB′​.
  2. 2.2Determine vB′​ and the mass mB​.
Part 2 · 2.75 pts

Part 3 — After the collision

(B) reaches the point N, then climbs a frictionless ramp. The horizontal plane of the table is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

  1. 3.1Calculate the maximum height h reached by (B) on the ramp.
  2. 3.2Calculate the variation ΔPA​ of the linear momentum of (A) during the collision. Deduce the average force exerted by (B) on (A), knowing that the contact lasts 10 ms.
Part 3 · 2.25 pts

Exercise 2 — A pendulum hits a block7 pts

A pendulum (P) is formed of a light inextensible string of length L=0.9 m and a small ball of mass m=200 g. It is released without initial velocity from A₀, the string making the angle θ0​=60∘ with the vertical (Doc. 2). At its lowest position O, the ball hits a block (B), of the same mass m, at rest on a horizontal table. Air resistance is neglected.

The horizontal plane through O is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — The descent

  1. 1.1Calculate the mechanical energy of the system [(P), Earth] at A₀.
  2. 1.2Deduce the speed v1​ of the ball just before hitting (B).
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — The elastic collision

The collision is head-on and perfectly elastic. Let v1′​ and v2′​ be the algebraic values of the velocities of the ball and of (B) just after the collision.

  1. 2.1Write the two equations that express the conservations during the collision.
  2. 2.2Show that the ball stops and that (B) moves with the speed v2′​=v1​.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — (B) on the rough table

(B) slides on the table, acted upon by a constant friction force f​, and stops after covering the distance d=1.5 m.

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation of the mechanical energy of the system [(B), Earth]. Deduce the magnitude f.
  2. 3.2Using Newton's second law ΣF=dtdP​, calculate the duration of the motion of (B) on the table.
Part 3 · 1.25 pts

Exercise 3 — A bouncing ball6 pts

A ball of mass m=0.5 kg is released without initial velocity from a height H=1.8 m above the ground. It bounces on the ground and rises vertically. The vertical axis (O,k) is directed upwards, O on the ground. The ground is the reference level of gravitational potential energy. Air resistance is neglected.

Part 1 — The fall

  1. 1.1Using the conservation of mechanical energy, calculate the speed v1​ of the ball just before it hits the ground.
Part 1 · 1 pts

Part 2 — The bounce

The ball leaves the ground with the speed v2​=4 m/s. The contact with the ground lasts Δt=0.05 s.

  1. 2.1Calculate the variation ΔP of the linear momentum of the ball during the contact.
  2. 2.2Deduce the average force exerted by the ground on the ball.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — After the bounce

  1. 3.1Calculate the kinetic energy lost during the bounce. Is the collision with the ground elastic? Justify.
  2. 3.2Calculate the maximum height h′ reached by the ball after the bounce.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts
