Energy & Momentum — Practice Exam 3

Three exercises on Energy and Linear Momentum, medium level, official-exam format, with a focus on perfectly elastic collisions. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This exam is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — A child on a swing7 pts

A child on a swing is modelled by a simple pendulum: a light inextensible string of length L=2.5 m, fixed at C, carrying a particle of mass m=25 kg. The pendulum is moved away from its equilibrium position O by an angle θ0​=60∘ (position A₀) and released without initial velocity. At an instant t, the string makes an angle θ with the vertical and the speed of the child is v (Doc. 1).

The horizontal plane through O is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.
cos30∘≈0.87; cos53∘=0.6.

Part 1 — Expressions

  1. 1.1Show that the gravitational potential energy of the system [pendulum, Earth] at M is GPE=mgL(1−cosθ).
  2. 1.2Write the expression of the mechanical energy of the system at M in terms of m, v, g, L and θ.
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — Ideal swing

In this part, friction is neglected.

  1. 2.1Calculate the mechanical energy of the system at A₀.
  2. 2.2Deduce the speed of the child at O and at the position where θ=30∘.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — Real swing

In reality, after passing through O, the child stops on the other side at the angle θ1​=53∘. This half-oscillation lasts 1.6 s.

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation of the mechanical energy of the system during this half-oscillation.
  2. 3.2Deduce the variation ΔU of the internal energy of the system [swing, air, Earth] and the average power of the friction forces.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts

Exercise 2 — Dot prints of an elastic collision7 pts

In a lab session, students study a collision on a horizontal air table. A puck (A) of mass mA​=300 g is launched toward a puck (B) of mass mB​=100 g, initially at rest. The collision is head-on and perfectly elastic. A spark recorder marks the positions of the centres of the pucks at successive equal time intervals τ=40 ms (Doc. 2). Friction is neglected.

Part 1 — Reading the recording

  1. 1.1Justify, using Doc. 2, that the motion of (A) before the collision is uniform rectilinear.
  2. 1.2Calculate the speed VA​ of (A) before the collision.
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — Predicting the velocities after the collision

Let VA′​=VA′​i and VB′​=VB′​i be the velocities of (A) and (B) just after the collision.

  1. 2.1Write the two equations that express the conservations during this collision.
  2. 2.2Determine the values of VA′​ and VB′​.
Part 2 · 2.75 pts

Part 3 — Checking with the recording

  1. 3.1Using Doc. 2, calculate the speed of (B) after the collision. Compare it with the value found in 2.2.
  2. 3.2The contact between the two pucks lasts 20 ms. Calculate the average force exerted by (A) on (B) during the collision.
Part 3 · 2.25 pts

Exercise 3 — A crane on a building site6 pts

A crane lifts a load of mass m=200 kg vertically, from the ground to a height h=10 m, at a constant speed v=0.5 m/s (Doc. 3). The load is acted upon by its weight and by the tension T of the cable; air resistance is neglected. The ground is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — Forces and energy

  1. 1.1Show that the linear momentum of the load stays constant during the lift. Deduce, using Newton's second law, the magnitude of T.
  2. 1.2Calculate the variation of the mechanical energy of the system [load, Earth] during the lift.
Part 1 · 3 pts

Part 2 — Power

  1. 2.1Calculate the duration of the lift. Deduce the power supplied by the cable to the load.
  2. 2.2The electric motor of the crane receives a power of 1250 W. Calculate its efficiency.
Part 2 · 3 pts
