Energy — Test Preparation 1

The drop tower · A counterweight on stage. Duration: 1 h 30 min · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This test is formed of two obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — The drop tower10 pts

In an amusement park, a gondola carrying its passengers is lifted to the top A of a vertical tower, then released from rest. It falls freely from A to B. Between B and C, magnetic brakes fixed on the tower exert on the gondola a force F of constant magnitude, opposite to its velocity, until it stops at C (Doc. 1). A data logger on board gives the energies of the system [gondola, Earth] during the descent (Doc. 2).

Mass of the gondola with its passengers: M=500 kg; it is considered as a particle.
Heights above the ground: zA​=60 m, zB​=40 m, zC​=15 m.
The position of the gondola is located by its abscissa x on the vertical axis (A,i) directed downward; v is the algebraic value of its velocity.
The ground is the reference level for gravitational potential energy. Air resistance is neglected in Parts 1 and 2.

Part 1 — The free fall (from A to B)

  1. 1.1Express the gravitational potential energy of the system in terms of M, g and x. Identify, with justification, the curve of Doc. 2 that represents it.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 2, show that the mechanical energy of the system is conserved between A and B. Justify this conservation.
  3. 1.3By deriving the mechanical energy with respect to time, determine the acceleration a1​ of the gondola. Specify the nature of its motion.
  4. 1.4Write the time equations v(t) and x(t) of the gondola between A and B. Deduce the instant tB​ at which it passes through B and its speed vB​ there.
Part 1 · 3.75 pts

Part 2 — The magnetic braking (from B to C)

  1. 2.1Using Doc. 2, determine the variation of the mechanical energy of the system between B and C. Deduce the variation of the internal energy of the system [gondola, brakes, Earth].
  2. 2.2Express the mechanical energy of the system between B and C in terms of x, the mechanical energy at B and F. Deduce the value of F.
  3. 2.3By deriving the mechanical energy with respect to time, determine the acceleration a2​ of the gondola. Specify the nature of its motion.
  4. 2.4Determine the duration of the braking, then the total duration of the descent from A to C.
  5. 2.5The designers require that the magnitude of the deceleration of the passengers never exceeds 2g. Is this requirement satisfied?
Part 2 · 3.75 pts

Part 3 — The real ride

In reality, the air exerts on the gondola a resistive force of constant magnitude f during the whole descent. The data logger now gives the kinetic energy KEB​=9.0×104 J at B.

  1. 3.1Determine f.
  2. 3.2Determine the new acceleration of the gondola between A and B and the instant at which it reaches B (360​≈19).
  3. 3.3The brakes are unchanged. Show that the gondola now stops 5.0 m above C.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts

Exercise 2 — Behind the curtain: a counterweight on stage10 pts

In a theatre, a heavy scenery wagon (W) is moved across the stage by a counterweight (C) that falls in a vertical shaft. The rope passes over a pulley at the edge of the stage (Doc. 3). At t0​=0, the system is released from rest. When (C) lands on the cushioned floor of the shaft, the rope becomes slack; (W) continues its motion and is finally stopped by a spring bumper.

Masses: (W) m1​=200 kg; (C) m2​=100 kg. Both are considered as particles.
At t0​=0, (C) is at the height h=1.5 m above the floor of the shaft; the stage is at the height H=2.5 m above this floor.
The stage exerts on (W) a friction force of constant magnitude f=100 N. The rope is inextensible; the rope and the pulley have negligible masses, and the pulley turns without friction. While the rope is taut, (W) and (C) have the same speed v.
The position of (W) is located by its abscissa x on the horizontal axis (O,i), O being its initial position.
The floor of the shaft is the reference level for gravitational potential energy.

A sensor gives the speed of (W) during the first second (Doc. 4).

Part 1 — The counterweight pulls (0≤x≤h)

  1. 1.1Write the expression of the mechanical energy of the system S= [(W), (C), rope, Earth] in terms of m1​, m2​, g, H, h, x and v.
  2. 1.2Is the mechanical energy of S conserved? Justify.
  3. 1.3By an energetic study, determine the expression of the acceleration a of (W) in terms of m1​, m2​, g and f. Calculate a and specify the nature of the motion of (W).
  4. 1.4Verify the value of a using Doc. 4.
  5. 1.5Determine the instant t1​ at which (C) reaches the floor and the speed v1​ of (W) at this instant. Verify v1​ by an energetic method.
  6. 1.6Calculate the variation of the internal energy of the system [S, stage] during this phase.
Part 1 · 4 pts

Part 2 — The wagon alone (h≤x≤h+D, with D=5.0 m)

(C) stops on the cushion as soon as it touches it; the rope becomes slack.

  1. 2.1Calculate the energy transformed into internal energy when (C) lands.
  2. 2.2By an energetic study of the system [(W), Earth], determine the acceleration a′ of (W). Specify the nature of its motion.
  3. 2.3Determine the speed v2​ with which (W) reaches the bumper, and the duration of this phase.
Part 2 · 2 pts

Part 3 — The spring bumper

The bumper behaves as a horizontal spring of negligible mass and of stiffness k=1.2×104 N/m. At an instant, the spring is compressed by X. The friction of the stage still acts; its maximum value when (W) is at rest is also f.

  1. 3.1Write the expression of the mechanical energy of the system [(W), spring, Earth]. By deriving it with respect to time, determine the expression of the acceleration of (W) in terms of f, k, X and m1​. Specify the nature of the motion.
  2. 3.2Show that the maximum compression of the spring is Xm​=25 cm.
  3. 3.3Calculate the acceleration of (W) at the instant it touches the bumper and at the maximum compression.
  4. 3.4Show that (W) does not remain at rest at the maximum compression. Determine its speed when it leaves the bumper, then the distance it travels and the time it takes before it stops.
  5. 3.5Show that the total variation of internal energy, from the release to the final stop, is equal to m2​gh. Interpret this result.
Part 3 · 4 pts
