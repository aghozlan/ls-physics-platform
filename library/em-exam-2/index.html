Energy & Momentum — Practice Exam 2

Three exercises covering Energy and Linear Momentum, medium level, official-exam format. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This exam is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — A ball thrown upwards7 pts

A ball (B), considered as a particle of mass m=100 g, is thrown vertically upwards from a point O with a speed v0​. An appropriate device gives, during the ascent, the kinetic energy KE of (B) and the gravitational potential energy GPE of the system [(B), Earth] as functions of the altitude z of (B) above O (Doc. 1). Air resistance is neglected in Parts 1 and 2.

The horizontal plane through O is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — Reading the graph

  1. 1.1Identify, with justification, the curve that represents KE and the one that represents GPE.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 1, calculate the speed v0​ and give the maximum altitude h reached by (B).
Part 1 · 2.25 pts

Part 2 — The mechanical energy

  1. 2.1Calculate the mechanical energy of the system [(B), Earth] at O and at the highest point. Conclude.
  2. 2.2Calculate the speed of (B) when it passes through the altitude z=0.8 m.
Part 2 · 2.25 pts

Part 3 — With air resistance

In reality, (B), thrown with the same speed v0​, rises only to the altitude h′=1 m. The air resistance is a force f​ of constant magnitude, opposite to the motion.

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation of the mechanical energy of the system [(B), Earth] between O and the new highest point. Deduce the magnitude f.
  2. 3.2Deduce the variation ΔU of the internal energy of the system [(B), air, Earth] during the ascent.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts

Exercise 2 — Collision on an air track7 pts

On a horizontal air track, a glider (A) of mass mA​=400 g, moving with the velocity VA​=0.6i (m/s), collides head-on with a glider (B) of mass mB​=200 g, initially at rest (Doc. 2). Friction is neglected.

Part 1 — Before the collision

  1. 1.1Show that the system [(A), (B)] is pseudo-isolated.
  2. 1.2Calculate the linear momentum P of the system before the collision.
Part 1 · 2 pts

Part 2 — After the collision

Just after the collision, (B) moves with the velocity VB′​=0.8i (m/s).

  1. 2.1Determine the velocity VA′​ of (A) just after the collision.
  2. 2.2Show that the collision is elastic.
Part 2 · 2.5 pts

Part 3 — The force during the collision

Doc. 3 shows the magnitude P of the linear momentum of (B) during the collision, which lasts 40 ms.

  1. 3.1Using Doc. 3, calculate the force FA/B​ exerted by (A) on (B) during the collision, supposed constant.
  2. 3.2Deduce the force FB/A​ exerted by (B) on (A). Name the law used.
Part 3 · 2.5 pts

Exercise 3 — Measuring the speed of a bullet6 pts

To measure the speed of a bullet (b) of mass m=10 g, it is fired horizontally into a wooden block (B) of mass M=1.99 kg, hanging at rest from light strings (Doc. 4). The bullet, moving with the velocity v=vi where v=400 m/s, gets embedded in the block, and the block rises. Air resistance is neglected.

Part 1 — The impact

  1. 1.1Calculate the linear momentum of the bullet just before the impact.
  2. 1.2The system [(b), (B)] is pseudo-isolated during the impact. Determine the speed V of the block (with the bullet inside) just after the impact.
Part 1 · 2.75 pts

Part 2 — Energy

The horizontal plane through the initial position of the centre of mass of (B) is the reference level of gravitational potential energy.

  1. 2.1Calculate the kinetic energy of the system [(b), (B)] just before and just after the impact. Deduce the variation of the internal energy of the system.
  2. 2.2Using the conservation of the mechanical energy of the system [(b) + (B), Earth], determine the height h by which the block rises.
Part 2 · 3.25 pts
