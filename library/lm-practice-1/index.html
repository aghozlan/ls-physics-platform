Linear Momentum — Practice Sheet

Three exam-level exercises: air-track collision, fireworks shell, curling shot. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This sheet is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: g=10 m/s2.

Exercise 1 — Testing bumpers on an air track6 pts

In a laboratory, two gliders (A) and (B) are launched towards each other on a horizontal air track. Each glider carries, on the facing side, a light spring bumper, so that the two gliders bounce off each other without any permanent deformation (Doc. 1).

Masses: mA​=300 g; mB​=200 g. Friction is negligible.
The centres of mass GA​ and GB​ of the gliders move along the axis (O,i); xA​ and xB​ are their abscissae.
The bumpers touch at the instant t=0.40 s. The duration of the contact is Δt=20 ms; it is neglected in Doc. 2.
Doc. 2 gives the abscissae of GA​ and GB​ at several instants.

Part 1 — The collision

  1. 1.1Show that the system [(A), (B)] is pseudo-isolated.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 2, show that each glider moves with a constant velocity before the collision and after it. Calculate the algebraic values vA​, vB​ of the velocities before the collision and vA′​, vB′​ after it.
  3. 1.3Determine the linear momentum P of the system before the collision and P′ after it. Conclude.
  4. 1.4Show that the collision is elastic.
Part 1 · 3.25 pts

Part 2 — The centre of mass of the system

  1. 2.1Write the expression of the abscissa xG​ of the centre of mass G of the system in terms of mA​, mB​, xA​ and xB​. Calculate xG​ at the instants 0, 0.40 s and 0.80 s.
  2. 2.2Deduce the nature of the motion of G and the value of its velocity vG​. Verify that P=(mA​+mB​)vG​.
Part 2 · 1.25 pts

Part 3 — During the contact

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation of the linear momentum of each glider during the contact. Compare these two variations.
  2. 3.2Taking ΔtΔP​ as equal to dtdP​, determine the average force FB/A​ exerted by (B) on (A) and the average force FA/B​ exerted by (A) on (B). Which principle is verified?
  3. 3.3At an instant of the contact, the bumpers are maximally compressed and the two gliders have the same velocity. Justify that this common velocity is vG​, then determine the maximum elastic potential energy stored in the bumpers.
Part 3 · 1.5 pts

Exercise 2 — The fireworks shell7 pts

During a celebration, a fireworks shell is fired from a mortar placed on the ground at O. It rises along a curved path and, at the highest point S, it explodes into two fragments (Doc. 3). Air resistance is neglected.

Mass of the shell: M=400 g; the shell and its fragments are considered as particles.
At t0​=0, the shell leaves O with the velocity v0​, of value v0​=30 m/s, making the angle α with the horizontal: sinα=0.8; cosα=0.6.
The motion is studied in the vertical plane (O,i,j​); the ground is the reference level for gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — Before the explosion

  1. 1.1Name the force acting on the shell after it leaves O. Is the shell an isolated system? Justify.
  2. 1.2Applying Newton's second law, show that the horizontal component Px​ of the linear momentum P of the shell is constant and that its vertical component is Py​=Mv0​sinα−Mgt. Calculate Px​ and the value of Py​ at t0​=0.
  3. 1.3Deduce the expressions of the components vx​ and vy​ of the velocity of the shell in terms of time. Determine the instant t1​ at which the shell reaches S, and its linear momentum PS​ at S.
  4. 1.4Knowing that vy​=dtdy​ and that y=0 at t0​=0, show that y=24t−5t2 (SI). Deduce the height H of S and its abscissa xS​. Verify H using the conservation of the mechanical energy of the system [shell, Earth].
Part 1 · 3.75 pts

Part 2 — The explosion at S

At S, the shell explodes into two fragments: F1​ of mass m1​=100 g and F2​ of mass m2​=300 g. Just after the explosion, F1​ has a zero velocity.

  1. 2.1The shell is not an isolated system. Explain why its linear momentum can nevertheless be considered as conserved during the explosion.
  2. 2.2Determine the velocity v2​ of F2​ just after the explosion (direction and value).
  3. 2.3Calculate the kinetic energy of the system just before and just after the explosion. What is the origin of the difference?
Part 2 · 1.75 pts

Part 3 — Where do the fragments land?

  1. 3.1Show that the two fragments reach the ground at the same instant, 2.4 s after the explosion.
  2. 3.2Determine the abscissae X1​ and X2​ of the points where F1​ and F2​ hit the ground.
  3. 3.3Show that the centre of mass of the two fragments reaches the ground at the point where the shell would have landed without explosion. Interpret this result using Newton's second law applied to the system [F1​, F2​].
Part 3 · 1.5 pts

Exercise 3 — A curling shot7 pts

Curling is played on a horizontal sheet of ice. A player releases his stone (A) at O; it slides in a straight line and hits, head-on, a stone (B) of the other team, at rest at a distance d=15 m from O (Doc. 4). The aim is to push (B) out and to keep (A) near the target, called the house.

The two stones have the same mass m=20 kg; they are considered as particles moving along the axis (O,i).
While a stone slides, the ice exerts on it a friction force f​ of constant magnitude f, opposite to its velocity.
Doc. 5 gives the algebraic value P of the linear momentum of (A) from its release at t0​=0 until it hits (B) at t1​=10 s.

Part 1 — The motion of (A) before the collision

  1. 1.1Name the external forces acting on (A) during its sliding.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 5, write the expression of P in terms of time. Applying Newton's second law, determine the magnitude f of the friction force.
  3. 1.3Deduce the expression of the algebraic value v of the velocity of (A) in terms of time and the value of its acceleration. Specify the nature of the motion of (A).
  4. 1.4Calculate the speed v1​ of (A) just before the collision. Verify, by applying the kinetic energy theorem, that (A) travels the distance d=15 m before hitting (B).
Part 1 · 3 pts

Part 2 — The collision

A video of the collision shows that the contact lasts Δt=2.0 ms and that, just after it, (A) keeps moving in the same direction with the speed vA′​=0.10 m/s.

  1. 2.1The system [(A), (B)] is subjected to friction. Justify that its linear momentum may be considered as conserved during the collision.
  2. 2.2Determine the velocity vB′​ of (B) just after the collision.
  3. 2.3Specify, with justification, the nature of the collision. What happens to the kinetic energy lost?
  4. 2.4Determine the average force exerted by (A) on (B) during the contact. Deduce the average force exerted by (B) on (A).
  5. 2.5Suppose now that the collision is elastic. Using the conservation of linear momentum and of kinetic energy, show that the two stones exchange their velocities.
Part 2 · 3.25 pts

Part 3 — Sweeping the ice

We go back to the real collision of Part 2. After it, (B) slides along (O,i); the centre of the house is 6.0 m beyond the point of collision.

  1. 3.1Determine the distance travelled by (B) until it stops. Does (B) reach the centre of the house?
  2. 3.2Players of the team of (B) sweep the ice in front of it, which reduces the friction force. Determine the magnitude of the friction force for which (B) stops exactly at the centre of the house.
Part 3 · 0.75 pts
