Energy — Test Preparation 2

The baggage chute · The rope swing over the river. Duration: 1 h 30 min · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This test is formed of two obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: \(g = 10\ \text{m/s}^2\).

Exercise 1 — The baggage chute10 pts

At an airport, suitcases slide down an inclined chute towards the reclaim area. At the lower end of the chute, a spring buffer stops them (Doc. 1). The chute exerts on a suitcase a friction force \(\vec f\) of constant magnitude, opposite to its velocity.

Mass of the suitcase (S): \(m = 20\ \text{kg}\); (S) is considered as a particle.
The chute is inclined by the angle \(\alpha\) with the horizontal: \(\sin\alpha = 0.5\); \(AB = 3.0\ \text{m}\).
Spring buffer: negligible mass, stiffness \(k = 8.0\times10^{2}\ \text{N/m}\), axis along the chute; its free end is at B when it has its natural length.
At \(t_0 = 0\), (S) is released without initial velocity at A. Its position is located by its abscissa \(x\) on the axis \((A,\vec i\,)\) directed down the chute; \(v\) is the algebraic value of its velocity.
The horizontal plane through B is the reference level for gravitational potential energy.
Doc. 2 represents, in terms of \(x\), the kinetic energy and the gravitational potential energy of the system [(S), Earth] between A and B.

Part 1 — Sliding down the chute (from A to B)

  1. 1.1Express the gravitational potential energy of the system [(S), Earth] in terms of \(m\), \(g\), \(\alpha\), \(AB\) and \(x\). Identify, with justification, the two curves of Doc. 2.
  2. 1.2Name the external forces acting on (S) between A and B, and give the expression of the work done by each of them between A and a position of abscissa \(x\).
  3. 1.3Applying the kinetic energy theorem between A and the position of abscissa \(x\), show that \(KE = (mg\sin\alpha - f)\,x\). Deduce, using Doc. 2, the value of \(f\).
  4. 1.4Express the mechanical energy of the system [(S), Earth] in terms of \(m\), \(g\), \(\alpha\), \(AB\), \(x\) and \(v\). By deriving the relation between this mechanical energy and the work of \(\vec f\) with respect to time, determine the acceleration \(a_1\) of (S). Specify the nature of its motion.
  5. 1.5Determine the instant \(t_B\) at which (S) reaches B and its speed \(v_B\) there. Verify \(v_B\) using Doc. 2.
  6. 1.6Calculate the variation of the internal energy of the system [(S), chute, Earth] between A and B.
Part 1 · 4.75 pts
1.1Height above B: \((AB - x)\sin\alpha \Rightarrow GPE = mg(AB - x)\sin\alpha = 300 - 100x\) (SI): curve (1) (decreasing, 300 J at A). Curve (2): \(KE\) (zero at A, increasing).0.75
1.2Weight: \(W = mgx\sin\alpha\); normal reaction: \(W = 0\) (perpendicular to the displacement); friction: \(W = -fx\).0.75
1.3\(KE - 0 = mgx\sin\alpha + 0 - fx = (mg\sin\alpha - f)x\). Doc. 2: slope of (2) \(= \tfrac{180}{3} = 60\ \text{J/m} = 100 - f \Rightarrow f = 40\ \text{N}\).1
1.4\(ME = \tfrac12mv^2 + mg(AB - x)\sin\alpha\); \(ME - ME_A = -fx\). Deriving (\(\tfrac{dx}{dt} = v\), \(\tfrac{dv}{dt} = a_1\)): \(mva_1 - mgv\sin\alpha = -fv \Rightarrow a_1 = g\sin\alpha - \tfrac{f}{m} = 5 - 2 = 3.0\ \text{m/s}^2\). Constant, same sign as \(v\): uniformly accelerated rectilinear motion.1.25
1.5\(x = 1.5t^2 = 3 \Rightarrow t_B = \sqrt2 \approx 1.4\ \text{s}\); \(v_B = 3t_B = 3\sqrt2 \approx 4.2\ \text{m/s}\). Doc. 2: \(KE_B = 180\ \text{J} = \tfrac12(20)v_B^2 \Rightarrow v_B^2 = 18\).0.75
1.6\(\Delta U = f\cdot AB = 120\ \text{J}\) (\(ME\): 300 J → 180 J).0.25

Part 2 — The spring buffer

(S) reaches the spring at B and compresses it; the friction force still acts. At an instant, the compression of the spring is \(X = x - AB\).

  1. 2.1Write the expression of the mechanical energy of the system [(S), spring, Earth] in terms of \(m\), \(g\), \(\alpha\), \(k\), \(X\) and \(v\). By deriving with respect to time, show that the acceleration of (S) is \(a = g\sin\alpha - \dfrac{f + kX}{m}\).
  2. 2.2Show that the speed of (S) keeps increasing just after it touches the spring. Determine the compression for which its speed is maximum, and calculate this maximum speed.
  3. 2.3Show that the maximum compression of the spring is \(X_m = 75\ \text{cm}\).
  4. 2.4Calculate the acceleration of (S) at the maximum compression. Describe the nature of the motion of (S) during the compression.
  5. 2.5The maximum value of the friction force when (S) is at rest is also \(f\). Show that (S) does not remain at rest at the maximum compression. Determine its speed when it leaves the spring, then the distance it travels back up the chute before it stops.
Part 2 · 5.25 pts
2.1Below B, \(GPE = -mgX\sin\alpha\): \(ME = \tfrac12mv^2 + \tfrac12kX^2 - mgX\sin\alpha\); \(ME - ME_B = -fX\). Deriving (\(\tfrac{dX}{dt} = v\)): \(mva + kXv - mgv\sin\alpha = -fv \Rightarrow a = g\sin\alpha - \tfrac{f + kX}{m}\).1.25
2.2At contact (\(X = 0\)): \(a = 3\ \text{m/s}^2 \gt 0\), same sign as \(v\): the speed still increases. Maximum when \(a = 0\): \(X = \tfrac{mg\sin\alpha - f}{k} = \tfrac{60}{800} = 7.5\ \text{cm}\). \(\tfrac12mv^2 = 180 + 60(0.075) - 400(0.075)^2 = 182.25\ \text{J} \Rightarrow v_{max} \approx 4.27\ \text{m/s}\).1
2.3\(0 - 180 + \tfrac12kX_m^2 - mgX_m\sin\alpha = -fX_m\): \(\tfrac12(800)(0.75)^2 = 225\ \text{J}\) and \(180 + (100 - 40)(0.75) = 225\ \text{J}\).0.75
2.4\(a = 5 - \tfrac{40 + 600}{20} = -27\ \text{m/s}^2\). Rectilinear motion with variable acceleration: accelerated from \(X = 0\) to 7.5 cm, then decelerated (not uniformly).0.75
2.5Up the chute: \(kX_m = 600\ \text{N} \gt mg\sin\alpha + f = 140\ \text{N}\): pushed back. Friction now acts down the chute: \(\tfrac12mv^2 = 225 - (100 + 40)(0.75) = 120\ \text{J} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{12} \approx 3.5\ \text{m/s}\). Going up: \(a = -(g\sin\alpha + f/m) = -7\ \text{m/s}^2\) (uniformly decelerated); distance \(= \tfrac{12}{14} \approx 0.86\ \text{m}\).1.5

Exercise 2 — The rope swing over the river10 pts

On a summer day at the river, a swimmer holds the end of a rope tied to a tree branch at O. Standing on a rock at A, the rope stretched and making the angle \(\theta_0\) with the vertical, he swings without initial velocity. At the lowest position B, he lets go of the rope and falls into the river (Doc. 3).

Mass of the swimmer: \(m = 60\ \text{kg}\); he is considered as a particle at the end of the rope.
The rope is inextensible and of negligible mass; \(OA = OB = L = 5.0\ \text{m}\); \(\theta_0 = 60^\circ\).
B is at the height \(h = 3.2\ \text{m}\) above the surface of the water. Air resistance is neglected in Parts 1 and 2.
Take: \(\cos60^\circ = 0.50\); \(\cos34^\circ \approx 0.83\); \(\cos48^\circ \approx 0.67\); \(\cos56^\circ \approx 0.56\).

Part 1 — The swing (from A to B)

The horizontal plane through B is the reference level for gravitational potential energy. At an instant, the rope makes the angle \(\theta\) with the vertical and the swimmer has the speed \(v\).

  1. 1.1Name the forces acting on the swimmer. Justify that the work done by the tension of the rope is zero.
  2. 1.2Applying the kinetic energy theorem between A and the position where the rope makes the angle \(\theta\), show that \(v^2 = 2gL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\). Calculate the speed \(v_B\) at B.
  3. 1.3Show that the mechanical energy of the system [swimmer, Earth] is conserved, and calculate its value.
  4. 1.4Determine the angle \(\theta_1\) at which the kinetic energy is twice the gravitational potential energy, and the speed of the swimmer at this position.
  5. 1.5Determine the angle \(\theta_2\) at which the gravitational potential energy is twice the kinetic energy.
Part 1 · 4.75 pts
1.1Weight \(m\vec g\) and tension \(\vec T\). \(\vec T\) is along the rope, perpendicular to the velocity (tangent to the circular path) at every instant: its work is zero.0.75
1.2Height lost from A to M: \(L\cos\theta - L\cos\theta_0\). \(\tfrac12mv^2 - 0 = mgL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0) + 0 \Rightarrow v^2 = 2gL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\). \(v_B = \sqrt{100\times0.5} = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.1\ \text{m/s}\).1.25
1.3\(ME = \tfrac12mv^2 + mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = mgL(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0) + mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = mgL(1 - \cos\theta_0) = 1500\ \text{J}\), independent of \(\theta\): conserved.0.75
1.4\(KE = 2GPE \Rightarrow ME = 3GPE\): \(mgL(1 - \cos\theta_1) = 500\ \text{J} \Rightarrow 1 - \cos\theta_1 = \tfrac16 \Rightarrow \cos\theta_1 \approx 0.83 \Rightarrow \theta_1 \approx 34^\circ\). \(KE = 1000\ \text{J} \Rightarrow v \approx 5.8\ \text{m/s}\).1.25
1.5\(GPE = 2KE \Rightarrow GPE = \tfrac23ME = 1000\ \text{J}\): \(1 - \cos\theta_2 = \tfrac13 \Rightarrow \cos\theta_2 \approx 0.67 \Rightarrow \theta_2 \approx 48^\circ\).0.75

Part 2 — The fall into the river

The swimmer lets go of the rope at B with the velocity \(\vec v_B\), horizontal. The surface of the water is now taken as the reference level for gravitational potential energy.

  1. 2.1Calculate the mechanical energy of the system [swimmer, Earth] at B with this new reference. Does the change of reference affect the speeds calculated in Part 1? Justify.
  2. 2.2Determine the speed of the swimmer when he reaches the water.
  3. 2.3Determine the height above the water at which his kinetic energy is twice the gravitational potential energy, and his speed at this height.
Part 2 · 2.75 pts
2.1\(ME_B = \tfrac12(60)(50) + 60\times10\times3.2 = 1500 + 1920 = 3420\ \text{J}\). No: \(ME\) changes by a constant, but only differences of \(GPE\) matter, so speeds are unchanged.0.75
2.2\(ME\) conserved: \(\tfrac12mv^2 = 3420\ \text{J} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{114} \approx 10.7\ \text{m/s}\).0.75
2.3\(GPE = \tfrac13ME = 1140\ \text{J} = mgz \Rightarrow z = 1.9\ \text{m}\); \(KE = 2280\ \text{J} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{76} \approx 8.7\ \text{m/s}\).1.25

Part 3 — The real swing

Another day, the swimmer does not let go of the rope. Because of air resistance, he stops on the other side when the rope makes the angle \(56^\circ\) with the vertical. The length of the path he follows between A and this position is about 10 m.

  1. 3.1Applying the kinetic energy theorem between A and this position, determine the work done by the air resistance.
  2. 3.2Deduce the average magnitude of the air resistance and the variation of the internal energy of the system [swimmer, air, Earth].
Part 3 · 2.5 pts
3.1\(0 - 0 = W_{weight} + W_T + W_{air}\); \(W_{weight} = mgL(\cos56^\circ - \cos60^\circ) = 3000(0.56 - 0.50) = 180\ \text{J}\); \(W_T = 0\) \(\Rightarrow W_{air} = -180\ \text{J}\).1.5
3.2\(f\times10 = 180 \Rightarrow f \approx 18\ \text{N}\). \(\Delta U = -\Delta ME = 180\ \text{J}\).1
