Linear Momentum — Practice Sheet

Three exam-level exercises: air-track collision, fireworks shell, curling shot. Duration: 2 hours · Total: 20 points. Solve each part on paper first, then open its solution.

This sheet is formed of three obligatory exercises. The use of a non-programmable calculator is allowed.
Take, in all exercises: \(g = 10\ \text{m/s}^2\).

Exercise 1 — Testing bumpers on an air track6 pts

In a laboratory, two gliders (A) and (B) are launched towards each other on a horizontal air track. Each glider carries, on the facing side, a light spring bumper, so that the two gliders bounce off each other without any permanent deformation (Doc. 1).

Masses: \(m_A = 300\ \text{g}\); \(m_B = 200\ \text{g}\). Friction is negligible.
The centres of mass \(G_A\) and \(G_B\) of the gliders move along the axis \((O,\vec i\,)\); \(x_A\) and \(x_B\) are their abscissae.
The bumpers touch at the instant \(t = 0.40\ \text{s}\). The duration of the contact is \(\Delta t = 20\ \text{ms}\); it is neglected in Doc. 2.
Doc. 2 gives the abscissae of \(G_A\) and \(G_B\) at several instants.
\(t\) (s)00.200.400.600.80
\(x_A\) (cm)010201816
\(x_B\) (cm)4035304356

Doc. 2

Part 1 — The collision

  1. 1.1Show that the system [(A), (B)] is pseudo-isolated.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 2, show that each glider moves with a constant velocity before the collision and after it. Calculate the algebraic values \(v_A\), \(v_B\) of the velocities before the collision and \(v'_A\), \(v'_B\) after it.
  3. 1.3Determine the linear momentum \(\vec P\) of the system before the collision and \(\vec P'\) after it. Conclude.
  4. 1.4Show that the collision is elastic.
Part 1 · 3.25 pts
1.1External forces: weights \(m_A\vec g\), \(m_B\vec g\) and the reactions of the air cushion, vertical, cancelling each other two by two; no friction. \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \vec 0\): pseudo-isolated system.0.5
1.2Equal displacements during equal time intervals (10 cm, 10 cm for A; 5 cm, 5 cm for B; then −2, −2 and 13, 13 cm): uniform rectilinear motions. \(v_A = \tfrac{0.10}{0.20} = 0.50\ \text{m/s}\); \(v_B = -\tfrac{0.05}{0.20} = -0.25\ \text{m/s}\); \(v'_A = -\tfrac{0.02}{0.20} = -0.10\ \text{m/s}\); \(v'_B = \tfrac{0.13}{0.20} = 0.65\ \text{m/s}\).1.25
1.3\(\vec P = (0.3\times0.50 + 0.2\times(-0.25))\,\vec i = 0.10\,\vec i\); \(\vec P' = (0.3\times(-0.10) + 0.2\times0.65)\,\vec i = 0.10\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s). \(\vec P' = \vec P\): the linear momentum of the pseudo-isolated system is conserved.0.75
1.4\(KE = \tfrac12(0.3)(0.5)^2 + \tfrac12(0.2)(0.25)^2 = 0.0375 + 0.00625 = 0.04375\ \text{J}\); \(KE' = \tfrac12(0.3)(0.1)^2 + \tfrac12(0.2)(0.65)^2 = 0.0015 + 0.04225 = 0.04375\ \text{J}\). \(KE' = KE\): elastic collision.0.75

Part 2 — The centre of mass of the system

  1. 2.1Write the expression of the abscissa \(x_G\) of the centre of mass G of the system in terms of \(m_A\), \(m_B\), \(x_A\) and \(x_B\). Calculate \(x_G\) at the instants \(0\), \(0.40\ \text{s}\) and \(0.80\ \text{s}\).
  2. 2.2Deduce the nature of the motion of G and the value of its velocity \(v_G\). Verify that \(\vec P = (m_A + m_B)\,\vec v_G\).
Part 2 · 1.25 pts
2.1\(x_G = \dfrac{m_Ax_A + m_Bx_B}{m_A + m_B}\). \(t = 0\): \(\tfrac{0 + 0.2(40)}{0.5} = 16\ \text{cm}\); \(t = 0.40\ \text{s}\): \(\tfrac{0.3(20) + 0.2(30)}{0.5} = 24\ \text{cm}\); \(t = 0.80\ \text{s}\): \(\tfrac{0.3(16) + 0.2(56)}{0.5} = 32\ \text{cm}\).0.75
2.2G covers 8 cm every 0.40 s, before and after the collision: uniform rectilinear motion, \(v_G = \tfrac{0.08}{0.40} = 0.20\ \text{m/s}\). \((m_A + m_B)v_G = 0.5\times0.20 = 0.10\ \text{kg·m/s} = P\) (verified).0.5

Part 3 — During the contact

  1. 3.1Calculate the variation of the linear momentum of each glider during the contact. Compare these two variations.
  2. 3.2Taking \(\dfrac{\Delta\vec P}{\Delta t}\) as equal to \(\dfrac{d\vec P}{dt}\), determine the average force \(\vec F_{B/A}\) exerted by (B) on (A) and the average force \(\vec F_{A/B}\) exerted by (A) on (B). Which principle is verified?
  3. 3.3At an instant of the contact, the bumpers are maximally compressed and the two gliders have the same velocity. Justify that this common velocity is \(\vec v_G\), then determine the maximum elastic potential energy stored in the bumpers.
Part 3 · 1.5 pts
3.1\(\Delta\vec P_A = 0.3(-0.10 - 0.50)\,\vec i = -0.18\,\vec i\); \(\Delta\vec P_B = 0.2(0.65 + 0.25)\,\vec i = 0.18\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s). \(\Delta\vec P_B = -\Delta\vec P_A\) (so \(\Delta\vec P_{system} = \vec 0\)).0.5
3.2On (A), the resultant is \(\vec F_{B/A}\) (weight and reaction cancel): \(\vec F_{B/A} = \tfrac{\Delta\vec P_A}{\Delta t} = \tfrac{-0.18}{0.020}\,\vec i = -9.0\,\vec i\ (\text{N})\); \(\vec F_{A/B} = \tfrac{0.18}{0.020}\,\vec i = 9.0\,\vec i\ (\text{N})\). \(\vec F_{A/B} = -\vec F_{B/A}\): principle of interaction (action and reaction).0.5
3.3Same velocity \(\vec u\): \(\vec P = (m_A + m_B)\,\vec u\); \(\vec P\) is conserved and equals \((m_A + m_B)\,\vec v_G\), so \(\vec u = \vec v_G\) (0.20 m/s). The mechanical energy of the system (gliders, bumpers) is conserved (elastic contact): \(EPE_{max} = 0.04375 - \tfrac12(0.5)(0.20)^2 = 0.04375 - 0.01 \approx 0.034\ \text{J}\).0.5

Exercise 2 — The fireworks shell7 pts

During a celebration, a fireworks shell is fired from a mortar placed on the ground at O. It rises along a curved path and, at the highest point S, it explodes into two fragments (Doc. 3). Air resistance is neglected.

Mass of the shell: \(M = 400\ \text{g}\); the shell and its fragments are considered as particles.
At \(t_0 = 0\), the shell leaves O with the velocity \(\vec v_0\), of value \(v_0 = 30\ \text{m/s}\), making the angle \(\alpha\) with the horizontal: \(\sin\alpha = 0.8\); \(\cos\alpha = 0.6\).
The motion is studied in the vertical plane \((O,\vec i,\vec j\,)\); the ground is the reference level for gravitational potential energy.

Part 1 — Before the explosion

  1. 1.1Name the force acting on the shell after it leaves O. Is the shell an isolated system? Justify.
  2. 1.2Applying Newton's second law, show that the horizontal component \(P_x\) of the linear momentum \(\vec P\) of the shell is constant and that its vertical component is \(P_y = Mv_0\sin\alpha - Mgt\). Calculate \(P_x\) and the value of \(P_y\) at \(t_0 = 0\).
  3. 1.3Deduce the expressions of the components \(v_x\) and \(v_y\) of the velocity of the shell in terms of time. Determine the instant \(t_1\) at which the shell reaches S, and its linear momentum \(\vec P_S\) at S.
  4. 1.4Knowing that \(v_y = \dfrac{dy}{dt}\) and that \(y = 0\) at \(t_0 = 0\), show that \(y = 24t - 5t^2\) (SI). Deduce the height \(H\) of S and its abscissa \(x_S\). Verify \(H\) using the conservation of the mechanical energy of the system [shell, Earth].
Part 1 · 3.75 pts
1.1Only the weight \(M\vec g\) (air resistance neglected). Not isolated: \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = M\vec g \ne \vec 0\).0.5
1.2\(\tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = M\vec g = -Mg\,\vec j\). Along \(\vec i\): \(\tfrac{dP_x}{dt} = 0 \Rightarrow P_x = \) constant \(= Mv_0\cos\alpha\). Along \(\vec j\): \(\tfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -Mg \Rightarrow P_y = -Mgt + Mv_0\sin\alpha\). \(P_x = 0.4\times30\times0.6 = 7.2\ \text{kg·m/s}\); \(P_y(0) = 0.4\times30\times0.8 = 9.6\ \text{kg·m/s}\).1.25
1.3\(v_x = \tfrac{P_x}{M} = 18\ \text{m/s}\); \(v_y = \tfrac{P_y}{M} = 24 - 10t\) (SI). At S the velocity is horizontal: \(v_y = 0 \Rightarrow t_1 = 2.4\ \text{s}\). \(\vec P_S = 7.2\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s).1
1.4\(y\) is a primitive of \(24 - 10t\): \(y = 24t - 5t^2 + C\), \(y(0) = 0 \Rightarrow C = 0\). \(H = y(2.4) = 57.6 - 28.8 = 28.8\ \text{m}\); \(x_S = v_xt_1 = 18\times2.4 = 43.2\ \text{m}\). Energy: \(\tfrac12Mv_0^2 = \tfrac12Mv_S^2 + MgH \Rightarrow H = \tfrac{30^2 - 18^2}{20} = 28.8\ \text{m}\) (verified).1

Part 2 — The explosion at S

At S, the shell explodes into two fragments: \(F_1\) of mass \(m_1 = 100\ \text{g}\) and \(F_2\) of mass \(m_2 = 300\ \text{g}\). Just after the explosion, \(F_1\) has a zero velocity.

  1. 2.1The shell is not an isolated system. Explain why its linear momentum can nevertheless be considered as conserved during the explosion.
  2. 2.2Determine the velocity \(\vec v_2\) of \(F_2\) just after the explosion (direction and value).
  3. 2.3Calculate the kinetic energy of the system just before and just after the explosion. What is the origin of the difference?
Part 2 · 1.75 pts
2.1The explosion is very short and the internal forces between the fragments are much larger than the weight; the effect of the weight during the explosion is negligible: \(\Delta\vec P = M\vec g\,\Delta t \approx \vec 0\).0.5
2.2\(\vec P_S = m_1\vec 0 + m_2\vec v_2 \Rightarrow \vec v_2 = \tfrac{7.2}{0.3}\,\vec i = 24\,\vec i\ (\text{m/s})\): horizontal, in the sense of \(\vec i\), \(v_2 = 24\ \text{m/s}\).0.75
2.3Before: \(\tfrac12(0.4)(18)^2 = 64.8\ \text{J}\); after: \(\tfrac12(0.3)(24)^2 = 86.4\ \text{J}\). Gain of 21.6 J, coming from the chemical (internal) energy of the explosive charge.0.5

Part 3 — Where do the fragments land?

  1. 3.1Show that the two fragments reach the ground at the same instant, \(2.4\ \text{s}\) after the explosion.
  2. 3.2Determine the abscissae \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) of the points where \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) hit the ground.
  3. 3.3Show that the centre of mass of the two fragments reaches the ground at the point where the shell would have landed without explosion. Interpret this result using Newton's second law applied to the system [\(F_1\), \(F_2\)].
Part 3 · 1.5 pts
3.1Each fragment starts at height \(H\) with a zero vertical velocity and is subjected only to its weight: \(\tfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -mg \Rightarrow v_y = -10t'\), \(y = H - 5t'^2\) for both (independent of the mass). \(y = 0 \Rightarrow t' = \sqrt{\tfrac{28.8}{5}} = 2.4\ \text{s}\) for both.0.5
3.2\(F_1\) falls vertically: \(X_1 = 43.2\ \text{m}\). \(F_2\): \(v_x = 24\ \text{m/s}\) constant: \(X_2 = 43.2 + 24\times2.4 = 100.8\ \text{m}\).0.5
3.3\(X_G = \tfrac{0.1(43.2) + 0.3(100.8)}{0.4} = 86.4\ \text{m}\). Without explosion (symmetric path): landing at \(2x_S = 86.4\ \text{m}\) (or \(y = 0\) at \(t = 4.8\ \text{s}\), \(x = 18\times4.8\)). For [\(F_1\), \(F_2\)]: \(\tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = (m_1 + m_2)\vec g = M\vec g\), the same law as for the shell, with the same \(\vec P\) at S: G continues the path of the shell.0.5

Exercise 3 — A curling shot7 pts

Curling is played on a horizontal sheet of ice. A player releases his stone (A) at O; it slides in a straight line and hits, head-on, a stone (B) of the other team, at rest at a distance \(d = 15\ \text{m}\) from O (Doc. 4). The aim is to push (B) out and to keep (A) near the target, called the house.

The two stones have the same mass \(m = 20\ \text{kg}\); they are considered as particles moving along the axis \((O,\vec i\,)\).
While a stone slides, the ice exerts on it a friction force \(\vec f\) of constant magnitude \(f\), opposite to its velocity.
Doc. 5 gives the algebraic value \(P\) of the linear momentum of (A) from its release at \(t_0 = 0\) until it hits (B) at \(t_1 = 10\ \text{s}\).

Part 1 — The motion of (A) before the collision

  1. 1.1Name the external forces acting on (A) during its sliding.
  2. 1.2Using Doc. 5, write the expression of \(P\) in terms of time. Applying Newton's second law, determine the magnitude \(f\) of the friction force.
  3. 1.3Deduce the expression of the algebraic value \(v\) of the velocity of (A) in terms of time and the value of its acceleration. Specify the nature of the motion of (A).
  4. 1.4Calculate the speed \(v_1\) of (A) just before the collision. Verify, by applying the kinetic energy theorem, that (A) travels the distance \(d = 15\ \text{m}\) before hitting (B).
Part 1 · 3 pts
1.1Weight \(m\vec g\), normal reaction \(\vec N\) of the ice, friction \(\vec f\).0.5
1.2Straight line: \(P = -2t + 40\) (SI). \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = -2\,\vec i\ (\text{N})\). \(m\vec g + \vec N = \vec 0\) (horizontal motion) \(\Rightarrow \vec f = -2\,\vec i\): \(f = 2.0\ \text{N}\), opposite to the motion.1
1.3\(v = \tfrac{P}{m} = -0.1t + 2\) (SI); \(a = \tfrac{dv}{dt} = -0.1\ \text{m/s}^2\), constant and of sign opposite to \(v\) (\(v \gt 0\) until 10 s): uniformly decelerated rectilinear motion.0.75
1.4\(v_1 = \tfrac{20}{20} = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\). KE theorem: \(\tfrac12m(v_1^2 - v_0^2) = W_{\vec f} = -fd\) (weight and \(\vec N\) do no work) \(\Rightarrow 10(1 - 4) = -2d \Rightarrow d = 15\ \text{m}\).0.75

Part 2 — The collision

A video of the collision shows that the contact lasts \(\Delta t = 2.0\ \text{ms}\) and that, just after it, (A) keeps moving in the same direction with the speed \(v'_A = 0.10\ \text{m/s}\).

  1. 2.1The system [(A), (B)] is subjected to friction. Justify that its linear momentum may be considered as conserved during the collision.
  2. 2.2Determine the velocity \(\vec v'_B\) of (B) just after the collision.
  3. 2.3Specify, with justification, the nature of the collision. What happens to the kinetic energy lost?
  4. 2.4Determine the average force exerted by (A) on (B) during the contact. Deduce the average force exerted by (B) on (A).
  5. 2.5Suppose now that the collision is elastic. Using the conservation of linear momentum and of kinetic energy, show that the two stones exchange their velocities.
Part 2 · 3.25 pts
2.1During 2.0 ms, the friction forces (about 2 N on each stone) are negligible compared with the forces of interaction (thousands of newtons, see 2.4): \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext}\,\Delta t \approx \vec 0\), the system is considered isolated during the shock.0.5
2.2\(mv_1 = mv'_A + mv'_B \Rightarrow v'_B = 1.0 - 0.10 = 0.90\ \text{m/s}\): \(\vec v'_B = 0.90\,\vec i\ (\text{m/s})\).0.5
2.3\(KE\) before \(= \tfrac12(20)(1)^2 = 10\ \text{J}\); after \(= \tfrac12(20)(0.10^2 + 0.90^2) = 8.2\ \text{J}\). \(KE\) not conserved: inelastic collision (not perfectly inelastic, the stones separate). The 1.8 J lost are transformed into internal energy of the stones (heating, vibrations) and sound.0.75
2.4\(\vec F_{A/B} = \tfrac{\Delta\vec P_B}{\Delta t} = \tfrac{20\times0.90}{2.0\times10^{-3}}\,\vec i = 9.0\times10^{3}\,\vec i\ (\text{N})\). Principle of interaction: \(\vec F_{B/A} = -9.0\times10^{3}\,\vec i\ (\text{N})\) (check: \(\Delta P_A = 20(0.10 - 1.0) = -18\)).0.75
2.5\(m v_1 = m v'_A + m v'_B \Rightarrow v'_A + v'_B = v_1\) (1); \(\tfrac12mv_1^2 = \tfrac12mv'^2_A + \tfrac12mv'^2_B \Rightarrow v'^2_A + v'^2_B = v_1^2\) (2). Squaring (1) and subtracting (2): \(2v'_Av'_B = 0\); (A) cannot pass through (B), so \(v'_A = 0\) and \(v'_B = v_1 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\): velocities exchanged.0.75

Part 3 — Sweeping the ice

We go back to the real collision of Part 2. After it, (B) slides along \((O,\vec i\,)\); the centre of the house is \(6.0\ \text{m}\) beyond the point of collision.

  1. 3.1Determine the distance travelled by (B) until it stops. Does (B) reach the centre of the house?
  2. 3.2Players of the team of (B) sweep the ice in front of it, which reduces the friction force. Determine the magnitude of the friction force for which (B) stops exactly at the centre of the house.
Part 3 · 0.75 pts
3.1KE theorem: \(0 - \tfrac12(20)(0.90)^2 = -2.0\,D \Rightarrow D = \tfrac{8.1}{2} \approx 4.1\ \text{m} \lt 6.0\ \text{m}\): (B) does not reach the centre.0.5
3.2\(0 - 8.1 = -f'\times6.0 \Rightarrow f' = 1.35\ \text{N}\).0.25
