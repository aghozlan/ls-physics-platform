Part 1 · From intuition to Newton's second law

What does it take to stop something?

Open each step, play with the live models, then check yourself with Cards 1 and Practice 1. Words in open a short explanation.

1

Feel it: who is harder to stop?

▶

Pick two moving objects and compare. Stopping a body is hard when it is heavy or when it is fast — the quantity that measures both together is the linear momentum.

Live model · Compare two moving objects
ThinkA bullet is light but very fast; a tanker is very slow but enormous. Which one has more momentum? Try it above.
2

The formula: a vector, not just a number

▶
DefinitionA particle of mass \(m\) moving with the velocity \(\vec v\) has the linear momentum \[\vec P = m\,\vec v\qquad(\text{unit: kg·m/s})\] \(\vec P\) has the direction and sense of \(\vec v\); its magnitude is \(P = mv\). In a frame \((O,\vec i,\vec j\,)\): \(P_x = m\,v_x\), \(P_y = m\,v_y\).
Live model · Drag the tip of the velocity
Watch the signA ball of 0.2 kg moving at 4 m/s towards the negative sense of the axis: \(\vec v = -4\,\vec i\), so \(\vec P = -0.8\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s). The minus sign carries the direction.
3

Linear momentum of a system

▶
SystemThe linear momentum of a system is the vector sum of the momenta of its parts: \[\vec P_{sys} = \vec P_1 + \vec P_2 + \dots = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 + \dots\]
Live model · Drag the two arrow tips · Challenge: make \(\vec P_{sys} = \vec 0\)
SurpriseTwo carts move towards each other: \(m_1 = 2\ \text{kg}\), \(\vec v_1 = 3\,\vec i\); \(m_2 = 3\ \text{kg}\), \(\vec v_2 = -2\,\vec i\). \(\vec P_{sys} = 6\,\vec i - 6\,\vec i = \vec 0\): both move, yet the system has zero momentum.
4

Centre of mass: a second road to the same momentum

▶
of two particles: \((m_1+m_2)\,\overrightarrow{OG} = m_1\,\overrightarrow{OG_1} + m_2\,\overrightarrow{OG_2}\)

Derive once with respect to time, then derive again:

position\(M\,\overrightarrow{OG} = m_1\overrightarrow{OG_1} + m_2\overrightarrow{OG_2}\)
d/dt →
velocity\(M\,\vec v_G = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2\)
d/dt →
acceleration\(M\,\vec a_G = \dfrac{d\vec P_{sys}}{dt}\)
Two approaches, one result\(\vec P_{sys} = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 = M\,\vec v_G\), with \(M = m_1 + m_2\). The second derivative opens the door to Newton's second law.
Live model · Watch G move while the two bodies move
5

Newton's second law

▶
LawIn a , the sum of the external forces acting on a system equals the time-derivative of its linear momentum: \[\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \frac{d\vec P}{dt}\] One particle of constant mass: \(\dfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = m\,\vec a\), so \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = m\,\vec a\). Internal forces cancel in pairs.
Live model · A constant force makes P grow linearly — slope = force

Which form do I write? Tap each situation.

\(\vec P(t) = (-0.9t + 0.4)\,\vec i\) is given.
A sled with a constant resultant force, between two instants.
A ball is kicked; the contact lasts 0.02 s.
A puck glides on an air table (forces cancel).
6

Isolated system: momentum is conserved

▶
DefinitionA system is isolated if no external force acts on it, or if the external forces cancel: \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \vec 0\). Then \(\dfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = \vec 0\): \(\vec P_{sys}\) is constant and G moves in a straight line at constant velocity (or stays at rest).

Isolated or not?

A puck on an air table
Two skaters on smooth ice
A ball in flight
A car braking on a road
Bonus — principle of interactionFor two bodies forming an isolated system: \(\tfrac{d\vec P_1}{dt} + \tfrac{d\vec P_2}{dt} = \vec 0\), so \(\vec F_{2\to1} = -\vec F_{1\to2}\).
7

Parabolic motion: two components, two behaviours

▶

A ball is thrown with \(\vec v_0\) at the angle \(\alpha\); air resistance is neglected. The only force is the weight \(m\vec g = -mg\,\vec j\).

Along x\(\dfrac{dP_x}{dt} = 0\Rightarrow P_x = m\,v_0\cos\alpha = \text{constant}\)
Along y\(\dfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -mg\Rightarrow P_y = -mg\,t + m\,v_0\sin\alpha\)
Live model · Launch and watch \(P_x\) and \(P_y\)
Reading the graphs (official-exam style)\(P_x(t)\) is a horizontal line; \(P_y(t)\) is a straight line of slope \(-mg\), so \(m = -\dfrac{\text{slope}}{g}\). At the top, \(P_y = 0\).

Exercise 1 — Two pucks on an air table6 pts

Two pucks (A) and (B), of masses \(m_A = 0.2\ \text{kg}\) and \(m_B = 0.3\ \text{kg}\), slide on a horizontal air table along the axis \((O,\vec i\,)\): \(x_A = 0.5\,t - 0.2\) and \(x_B = -0.2\,t + 0.6\) (SI).

  1. 1.1Determine \(\vec v_A\) and \(\vec v_B\). In which sense does each puck move?
    Velocity is the derivative of the position: \(v = \tfrac{dx}{dt}\).
    \(\vec v_A = 0.5\,\vec i\) (positive sense); \(\vec v_B = -0.2\,\vec i\) (negative sense), in m/s.
  2. 1.2Calculate \(\vec P_A\), \(\vec P_B\) and the linear momentum \(\vec P\) of the system (A, B).
    \(\vec P_A = 0.1\,\vec i\); \(\vec P_B = -0.06\,\vec i\); \(\vec P = 0.04\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s).
  3. 1.3Show that \(x_G = 0.08\,t + 0.28\) (SI). Deduce \(\vec v_G\), then verify that \(\vec P = (m_A + m_B)\,\vec v_G\).
    \(x_G = \dfrac{m_Ax_A + m_Bx_B}{m_A + m_B}\).
    \(x_G = \tfrac{0.2(0.5t-0.2) + 0.3(-0.2t+0.6)}{0.5} = \tfrac{0.04t + 0.14}{0.5} = 0.08t + 0.28\). \(\vec v_G = 0.08\,\vec i\); \(0.5\times0.08 = 0.04 = P\).
  4. 1.4Derive \(\vec P\) with respect to time. What do you conclude about the external forces? Name them and explain why they cancel.
    \(\tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = \vec 0 \Rightarrow \Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \vec 0\). Weights and air-cushion reactions cancel (vertical); no friction: pseudo-isolated system.
  5. 1.5Specify the nature of the motion of G.
    \(\vec v_G\) constant: uniform rectilinear motion at 0.08 m/s.

Exercise 2 — A sled in Mzaar, then a volleyball smash7 pts

Part A. A sled carrying a child (\(M = 40\ \text{kg}\)) slides down a straight snowy slope inclined by \(\alpha\) (\(\sin\alpha = 0.10\)). The axis \((O,\vec i\,)\) is along the slope, downward. The snow exerts a friction force \(\vec f\) of constant magnitude. A phone app gives \(\vec P = (20\,t + 40)\,\vec i\) (SI).

  1. A.1Name the external forces acting on the system (sled + child).
    Weight \(M\vec g\), normal reaction \(\vec N\), friction \(\vec f\).
  2. A.2Applying Newton's second law, determine \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext}\). Deduce \(f\).
    Project on \(\vec i\): only the weight component \(Mg\sin\alpha\) and \(-f\) remain.
    \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = 20\,\vec i\) (N). \(Mg\sin\alpha - f = 20 \Rightarrow f = 40 - 20 = 20\ \text{N}\).
  3. A.3Determine the velocity at \(t = 0\) and the acceleration. Specify the nature of the motion.
    \(\vec v = \vec P/M = (0.5t + 1)\,\vec i\): \(v_0 = 1\ \text{m/s}\); \(\vec a = 0.5\,\vec i\ \text{m/s}^2\), constant, same sense as \(\vec v\): uniformly accelerated rectilinear motion.
  4. A.4Calculate \(\Delta\vec P\) between \(t = 0\) and \(t = 4\ \text{s}\). Verify that \(\Delta\vec P = \Sigma\vec F_{ext}\,\Delta t\). Why is this allowed here?
    \(\Delta\vec P = 120\,\vec i - 40\,\vec i = 80\,\vec i\); \(\Sigma F\,\Delta t = 20\times4 = 80\). Allowed because \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext}\) is constant.

Part B. A volleyball (\(m = 0.27\ \text{kg}\)) arrives horizontally at 8 m/s and leaves in the opposite sense at 12 m/s. The contact lasts \(\Delta t = 0.02\ \text{s}\). Take \(\vec i\) in the sense of the final velocity.

  1. B.1Write \(\vec P_i\) and \(\vec P_f\); calculate \(\Delta\vec P\). (A student writes \(\Delta P = 0.27\times(12-8)\): find the mistake.)
    \(\vec P_i = -2.16\,\vec i\), \(\vec P_f = 3.24\,\vec i\), \(\Delta\vec P = 5.40\,\vec i\). The senses are opposite: \(\Delta v = 12 - (-8) = 20\ \text{m/s}\).
  2. B.2Calculate the average force of the hands on the ball. Compare with the weight and conclude.
    \(F_{avg} = 5.40/0.02 = 270\ \text{N}\) along \(\vec i\). Weight \(= 2.7\ \text{N}\): 100 times smaller, negligible during the shock.

Exercise 3 — A basketball shot7 pts

A player throws a basketball from the height \(h_0 = 2.0\ \text{m}\) at \(t_0 = 0\). Air resistance is neglected. A video analysis gives the components of \(\vec P\) (\(\vec j\) upward) in Doc. 1. The ball enters the basket at \(t = 0.7\ \text{s}\).

  1. 3.1Name the force acting on the ball during its flight. Is the ball an isolated system?
    Only the weight. Not isolated: \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = m\vec g \ne \vec 0\).
  2. 3.2Applying Newton's second law, show that \(P_x\) is constant and \(\tfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -mg\). Do graphs (a) and (b) agree?
    \(\tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = -mg\,\vec j\): \(\tfrac{dP_x}{dt} = 0\) (graph (a) horizontal), \(\tfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -mg\) constant (graph (b) a straight line).
  3. 3.3Using graph (b), determine the mass of the ball.
    Read two points of graph (b), compute the slope; slope \(= -mg\).
    Slope \(= \tfrac{-1.2 - 3.0}{0.7} = -6\ \text{kg·m/s}^2 = -mg \Rightarrow m = 0.6\ \text{kg}\).
  4. 3.4Determine the components of \(\vec v_0\), its magnitude and its angle \(\alpha\) with the horizontal (\(\cos 51^\circ \approx 0.63\)).
    \(v_{0x} = 2.4/0.6 = 4\ \text{m/s}\); \(v_{0y} = 3.0/0.6 = 5\ \text{m/s}\); \(v_0 = \sqrt{41} \approx 6.4\ \text{m/s}\); \(\cos\alpha = 4/6.4 \approx 0.63 \Rightarrow \alpha \approx 51^\circ\).
  5. 3.5When does the ball reach the top of its path? What is then its linear momentum?
    Top: \(P_y = 0\) at \(t = 0.5\ \text{s}\). \(\vec P = 2.4\,\vec i\) (horizontal, minimum).
  6. 3.6Determine \(\vec P\) and the speed when the ball enters the basket (\(h = 3.05\ \text{m}\)); check the speed by an energetic method.
    \(\vec P = 2.4\,\vec i - 1.2\,\vec j\); \(v = \sqrt{2.4^2 + 1.2^2}/0.6 \approx 4.5\ \text{m/s}\). Energy: \(v^2 = v_0^2 - 2g(3.05 - 2.0) = 41 - 21 = 20\).

Part 2 · Collisions and explosions

When bodies hit or break apart

During a very short interaction, momentum is the quantity you can trust.

8

Why momentum is conserved in a collision or an explosion

▶
Key ideaThe interaction lasts a very short time. The internal forces are huge; the external forces (weights, reactions, friction) are finite and act during a negligible time. The system is : \[\vec P_{sys}(\text{just before}) = \vec P_{sys}(\text{just after})\]
Method① System = all the bodies that interact. ② Choose an axis. ③ Write \(\vec P\) just before and just after. ④ Equate, then project (signs!).
TrapA ball bouncing on a wall: the wall (Earth) is external; the momentum of the ball alone is not conserved.

Explosion from rest

Live model · Choose the masses, then explode
Explosion from rest\(\vec 0 = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 \Rightarrow \vec v_2 = -\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}\vec v_1\): opposite senses; the lighter part is faster. G stays at rest. The kinetic energy comes from internal (chemical or elastic) energy.
9

Elastic collision — the main case

▶
ElasticThe linear momentum and the kinetic energy of the system are conserved. Head-on, \(m_2\) initially at rest: \[m_1v_1 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2'\qquad \tfrac12m_1v_1^2 = \tfrac12m_1v_1'^2 + \tfrac12m_2v_2'^2\]
Solving trick\(m_1(v_1 - v_1') = m_2v_2'\) and \(m_1(v_1^2 - v_1'^2) = m_2v_2'^2\). Divide: \(v_1 + v_1' = v_2'\). Then \[v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_1\qquad v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}v_1\]
CaseResult
\(m_1 = m_2\)Exchange: \(v_1' = 0\), \(v_2' = v_1\) (billiards, Newton's cradle)
\(m_1 \lt m_2\)\(v_1' \lt 0\): (1) bounces back
\(m_1 \gt m_2\)Both move forward
Live model · Collision lab — try each case of the table
“Show that the collision is elastic”(official exam 2021): calculate the kinetic energy of the system just before and just after, and show they are equal.
10

Inelastic collision — just know it

▶

Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not: part of it becomes internal energy (deformation, heat, sound). If the bodies stick together (perfectly inelastic, “soft” collision) they share a common velocity \(\vec V = \dfrac{m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2}{m_1 + m_2}\). Try “Stick together” in the lab above.

Exercise 1 — Fireworks and a hunting rifle6 pts

Part A. A firework shell (\(M = 1.2\ \text{kg}\)) is launched vertically. At the top of its path (velocity zero) it explodes into two fragments: (1) of mass \(m_1 = 0.4\ \text{kg}\), thrown horizontally with \(\vec v_1 = 30\,\vec i\) (m/s), and (2) of mass \(m_2\).

  1. A.1Why is the shell a pseudo-isolated system during the explosion, although subject to its weight?
    The explosion is very short; the internal forces are much larger than the weight, whose effect during that time is negligible.
  2. A.2What is \(\vec P\) just before the explosion? Determine \(\vec v_2\).
    \(\vec P = \vec 0\). \(m_2 = 0.8\ \text{kg}\): \(0.4\times30\,\vec i + 0.8\,\vec v_2 = \vec 0 \Rightarrow \vec v_2 = -15\,\vec i\) (m/s).
  3. A.3Calculate the kinetic energy gained. Where does it come from?
    \(\tfrac12(0.4)(30)^2 + \tfrac12(0.8)(15)^2 = 180 + 90 = 270\ \text{J}\), from the chemical (internal) energy of the powder.
  4. A.4What is the velocity of G just after the explosion?
    \(\vec v_G = \vec P/M = \vec 0\).

Part B. A hunting rifle (4.0 kg) fires a 10 g bullet at 400 m/s from rest.

  1. B.1Calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle held loosely.
    \(0.01\times400 + 4.0\,v = 0 \Rightarrow v = -1.0\ \text{m/s}\).
  2. B.2The hunter (70 kg) holds it firmly against his shoulder. New recoil velocity? Why is it safer?
    \(v = -4/74 \approx -0.05\ \text{m/s}\): the recoiling mass is much larger, so the recoil is much slower and softer.

Exercise 2 — Elastic collision on an air track8 pts

Glider \((S_1)\): \(m_1 = 0.2\ \text{kg}\); glider \((S_2)\): \(m_2 = 0.3\ \text{kg}\), at rest. Spring: \(k = 80\ \text{N/m}\), compressed by \(d = 5\ \text{cm}\) and released; \((S_1)\) leaves the spring before hitting \((S_2)\). No friction.
  1. 2.1Show that \((S_1)\) leaves the spring with \(v_1 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\).
    \(\tfrac12kd^2 = \tfrac12m_1v_1^2\): \(0.1\ \text{J} = 0.1\,v_1^2 \Rightarrow v_1 = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\).
  2. 2.2Why is the momentum of \((S_1, S_2)\) conserved during the collision?
    Weights and reactions cancel, no friction, short collision: pseudo-isolated system.
  3. 2.3The collision is elastic. Write the two conservation relations in terms of \(v_1'\) and \(v_2'\).
    \(0.2(1) = 0.2v_1' + 0.3v_2'\); \(\tfrac12(0.2)(1)^2 = \tfrac12(0.2)v_1'^2 + \tfrac12(0.3)v_2'^2\).
  4. 2.4Deduce \(v_1 + v_1' = v_2'\), then calculate \(v_1'\) and \(v_2'\). Interpret the sign of \(v_1'\).
    Write \(0.2(1 - v_1') = 0.3v_2'\) and \(0.2(1 - v_1'^2) = 0.3v_2'^2\), then divide.
    Dividing: \(1 + v_1' = v_2'\). Then \(0.2 - 0.2v_1' = 0.3 + 0.3v_1' \Rightarrow v_1' = -0.2\ \text{m/s}\), \(v_2' = 0.8\ \text{m/s}\). \((S_1)\) bounces back (\(m_1 \lt m_2\)).
  5. 2.5Verify numerically the conservation of \(\vec P\) and of the kinetic energy.
    \(P' = -0.04 + 0.24 = 0.2\ \text{kg·m/s}\); \(KE' = 0.004 + 0.096 = 0.1\ \text{J}\).
  6. 2.6Calculate the velocity of G before and after. Conclude.
    \(v_G = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4\ \text{m/s}\) before and after: the collision does not change the motion of G.
  7. 2.7\((S_2)\) is replaced by a 0.2 kg glider. Predict the velocities after an elastic collision.
    Equal masses: exchange, \(v_1' = 0\), \(v_2' = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\).
  8. 2.8Just to know. With Velcro, the gliders stick together. Common speed? Kinetic energy transformed into internal energy?
    \(V = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4\ \text{m/s}\); \(KE' = 0.04\ \text{J}\); lost \(0.06\ \text{J}\) (60 %).

Exercise 3 — Spot the interaction6 pts

For each situation: type (elastic, perfectly inelastic, explosion, or none), is the momentum of the indicated system conserved, and the missing value.

(a) Billiards. The white ball (170 g) at 2.0 m/s hits head-on the red ball (170 g) at rest; the white ball stops. System: the two balls.
Conserved: \(v = 2.0\ \text{m/s}\). \(KE\) = 0.34 J before and after: elastic (exchange).
(b) Dart. A 20 g dart at 10 m/s sticks into a 180 g cork block on a frictionless cart. System: dart + block.
Conserved: \(V = 1.0\ \text{m/s}\). \(KE\): 1.0 J → 0.1 J (0.9 J lost): perfectly inelastic.
(c) Ice rink. Two skaters (60 kg and 40 kg), at rest, push each other; the 40 kg skater leaves at 3.0 m/s. System: the two skaters.
Conserved: \(60v + 120 = 0 \Rightarrow v = -2.0\ \text{m/s}\): explosion type.
(d) Wall. A tennis ball (60 g) hits a wall perpendicularly at 20 m/s and bounces back at 20 m/s. System: the ball alone. \(\Delta\vec P\)? Conserved?
\(\Delta P = 1.2 - (-1.2) = 2.4\ \text{kg·m/s}\), away from the wall: not conserved (the wall exerts an external force). \(KE\) unchanged.
