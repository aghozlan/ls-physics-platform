Part 1 — From intuition to Newton's second law
1

Feel it: who is harder to stop?

Stopping a moving body is hard when it is heavy or when it is fast. Catching a basketball is easy; catching a bowling ball at the same speed is not. A 10 g bullet is light, but at 400 m/s it goes through wood. A loaded oil tanker drifting slowly needs kilometres to stop. One quantity measures this “quantity of motion”: the linear momentum.

2

The formula: a vector, not just a number

DefinitionA particle of mass \(m\) moving with the velocity \(\vec v\) has the linear momentum \[\vec P = m\,\vec v \qquad (\text{unit: kg·m/s})\] \(\vec P\) has the direction and sense of \(\vec v\); its magnitude is \(P = m v\).

In a frame \((O,\vec i,\vec j\,)\): \(P_x = m\,v_x\) and \(P_y = m\,v_y\).

Watch the sign. A ball of 0.2 kg moving at 4 m/s towards the negative sense of the axis: \(\vec v = -4\,\vec i\), so \(\vec P = -0.8\,\vec i\) (kg·m/s). The minus sign carries the direction.
3

Linear momentum of a system

SystemThe linear momentum of a system is the vector sum of the momenta of its parts: \[\vec P_{sys} = \vec P_1 + \vec P_2 + \dots = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 + \dots\]
Surprise. Two carts move towards each other: \(m_1 = 2\ \text{kg}\), \(\vec v_1 = 3\,\vec i\); \(m_2 = 3\ \text{kg}\), \(\vec v_2 = -2\,\vec i\) (m/s).
\(\vec P_{sys} = 6\,\vec i - 6\,\vec i = \vec 0\): both carts move, yet the system has zero momentum.
4

Centre of mass: a second road to the same momentum

Centre of mass G(also called centre of inertia) of two particles: \[(m_1+m_2)\,\overrightarrow{OG} = m_1\,\overrightarrow{OG_1} + m_2\,\overrightarrow{OG_2}\]

Derive once with respect to time, then derive again:

position\((m_1+m_2)\overrightarrow{OG} = m_1\overrightarrow{OG_1} + m_2\overrightarrow{OG_2}\)
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\) →
velocity\(M\vec v_G = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2\)
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\) →
acceleration\(M\vec a_G = \dfrac{d\vec P_{sys}}{dt}\)
Two approaches, one result\(\vec P_{sys} = \underbrace{m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2}_{\text{sum of the parts}} = \underbrace{M\,\vec v_G}_{\text{total mass}\,\times\,\text{velocity of G}}\), with \(M = m_1 + m_2\).
The second derivative links \(\vec P\) to the acceleration of G: this opens the door to Newton's second law.
5

Newton's second law

LawIn a Galilean (inertial) frame, the sum of the external forces acting on a system equals the time-derivative of its linear momentum: \[\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \frac{d\vec P}{dt}\] For one particle of constant mass: \(\dfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = m\dfrac{d\vec v}{dt} = m\,\vec a\), so \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = m\,\vec a\). Internal forces cancel in pairs and never appear.
SituationWhat you write
\(\vec P(t)\) is given as a function of timeDerive: \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \dfrac{d\vec P}{dt}\). Official exam 2021: \(\vec P = (-0.9t + 0.4)\,\vec i \Rightarrow \Sigma\vec F = -0.9\,\vec i\ \text{(N)}\).
\(\Sigma\vec F_{ext}\) is constant\(\vec P\) varies linearly with \(t\), so the derivative becomes a ratio of differences: \(\Delta\vec P = \vec P_f - \vec P_i = \Sigma\vec F_{ext}\,\Delta t\).
Very short shock (kick, bounce)Average force: \(\Sigma\vec F_{avg} = \dfrac{\Delta\vec P}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{\vec P_f - \vec P_i}{\Delta t}\) — subtract vectors, signs included.
\(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \vec 0\)\(\Delta\vec P = \vec 0\): \(\vec P_f = \vec P_i\). The momentum is conserved (step 6).
6

Isolated system: momentum is conserved

DefinitionA system is isolated if it is subject to no external force, or pseudo-isolated if the external forces cancel: \(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \vec 0\).
Then \(\dfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = \vec 0\): \(\vec P_{sys} = \overrightarrow{\text{constant}}\), and G moves in a straight line at constant velocity (or stays at rest).
Isolated (pseudo-isolated)?Why
Puck on an air table — yesWeight and air-cushion reaction cancel; no friction.
Two skaters on smooth ice — yesWeights and normal reactions cancel; friction neglected.
Ball in flight — noThe weight is not balanced.
Car braking on a road — noThe friction of the road is an external force.
Bonus — principle of interaction. For two bodies forming an isolated system: \(\tfrac{d\vec P_1}{dt} + \tfrac{d\vec P_2}{dt} = \vec 0\), so \(\vec F_{2\to1} = -\vec F_{1\to2}\).
7

Parabolic motion: two components, two behaviours

A ball is thrown with \(\vec v_0\) at the angle \(\alpha\) above the horizontal; air resistance is neglected. The only force is the weight \(m\vec g = -mg\,\vec j\).

Along x\(\dfrac{dP_x}{dt} = 0\)
\(P_x = m\,v_0\cos\alpha = \text{constant}\)
\(v_x = v_0\cos\alpha\)
Along y\(\dfrac{dP_y}{dt} = -mg\)
\(P_y = -mg\,t + m\,v_0\sin\alpha\)
\(v_y = -g\,t + v_0\sin\alpha\)
Reading the graphs (official-exam style). \(P_x(t)\) is a horizontal line; \(P_y(t)\) is a straight line of slope \(-mg\), so \(m = -\dfrac{\text{slope}}{g}\). At the top of the path \(P_y = 0\), and \(P = P_x\) is minimum.

\(\vec P = m\vec v\) (kg·m/s). Same direction and sense as \(\vec v\). Magnitude \(P = mv\).

2Components

\(P_x = mv_x\), \(P_y = mv_y\). Along one axis, the sign gives the sense: \(\vec P = -0.8\,\vec i\) points backwards.

3System

4Centre of mass

derive: \(\vec P_{sys} = M\vec v_G\)
derive again: \(\tfrac{d\vec P}{dt} = M\vec a_G\)

\(\Sigma\vec F_{ext} = \dfrac{d\vec P}{dt}\). Only external forces. One particle: \(\Sigma\vec F = m\vec a\).

\(\vec P(t)\) given → derive. Constant force → \(\Delta\vec P = \Sigma\vec F\,\Delta t\). Shock → \(\vec F_{avg} = \Delta\vec P/\Delta t\).

Part 2 — Collisions and explosions
8

Why momentum is conserved in a collision or an explosion

Key ideaA collision or an explosion lasts a very short time. The internal forces between the bodies are huge; the external forces (weights, reactions, friction) are finite and act during a negligible time. During the interaction, the system is pseudo-isolated: \[\vec P_{sys}(\text{just before}) = \vec P_{sys}(\text{just after})\]
Method (4 steps)
① System = all the bodies that interact. ② Choose an axis. ③ Write \(\vec P\) just before and just after. ④ Equate, then project on the axis (signs!).
Trap. A ball bouncing on a wall: the wall (Earth) is an external body; the momentum of the ball alone is not conserved.

Explosion — a body at rest breaks into two parts (fireworks, rifle recoil, two skaters pushing each other).

Explosion from rest\(\vec 0 = m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 \Rightarrow \vec v_2 = -\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}\,\vec v_1\): opposite senses, and the lighter part is the faster. G stays at rest. Kinetic energy appears: it comes from internal (chemical or elastic) energy.
9

Elastic collision — the main case

ElasticBoth the linear momentum and the kinetic energy of the system are conserved. Head-on collision, \(m_2\) initially at rest: \[m_1v_1 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2' \qquad\qquad \tfrac12m_1v_1^2 = \tfrac12m_1v_1'^2 + \tfrac12m_2v_2'^2\]
Solving trick. Rewrite: \(m_1(v_1 - v_1') = m_2v_2'\) and \(m_1(v_1^2 - v_1'^2) = m_2v_2'^2\). Divide: \(v_1 + v_1' = v_2'\). Then: \[v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1 \qquad v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1\]
CaseResult
\(m_1 = m_2\)Exchange: \(v_1' = 0\), \(v_2' = v_1\) (billiards, Newton's cradle)
\(m_1 \lt m_2\)\(v_1' \lt 0\): (1) bounces back
\(m_1 \gt m_2\)Both move forward
“Show that the collision is elastic” (official exam 2021): calculate the kinetic energy of the system just before and just after, and show they are equal.
10

Inelastic collision — just know it

Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not: part of it becomes internal energy (deformation, heat, sound). If the bodies stick together (perfectly inelastic or “soft” collision), they share a common velocity: \(\vec V = \dfrac{m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2}{m_1 + m_2}\).

9When is \(\vec P\) conserved?

During a collision or an explosion (very short): system pseudo-isolated → \(\vec P_{before} = \vec P_{after}\).

10Method

System = all interacting bodies · axis · \(\vec P\) just before = \(\vec P\) just after · project with signs.

11Explosion from rest

\(m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2 = \vec 0\): opposite senses; \(\tfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \tfrac{m_2}{m_1}\). KE comes from internal energy.

12Elastic collision

\(\vec P\) conserved and \(KE\) conserved. Check: \(KE_{before} = KE_{after}\).

13Head-on, target at rest

\(v_1' = \tfrac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}v_1\), \(v_2' = \tfrac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}v_1\). Equal masses: exchange of velocities.

14Inelastic

\(\vec P\) conserved, \(KE\) not. Stuck together: \(\vec V = \tfrac{m_1\vec v_1 + m_2\vec v_2}{m_1+m_2}\).
