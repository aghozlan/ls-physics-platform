Everything that wobbles, ticks or bounces

A heartbeat, a guitar string, a car on its springs, the quartz in a watch: they all repeat the same motion again and again. Oscillations around us — and the 3D lab

▶

An oscillation is a to-and-fro motion about a stable equilibrium position, between two extreme positions. Pull the glider below and let it go: you will see the three numbers that describe every oscillation.

3D lab · Horizontal elastic pendulum on an air track

Drag the glider (mouse or finger) to stretch or compress the spring, then let go. With a mouse, drag the background to turn around the scene. The orange marks show ±Xm.

Vocabulary Amplitude Xm​: the largest distance from equilibrium (m). Period T: duration of one complete oscillation (s). Frequency f=T1​: number of oscillations per second (Hz).
Free oscillations: after being released, the oscillator receives no more energy. They are undamped (constant amplitude, the ideal case, no friction) or damped (the amplitude decreases because of friction).

2

The horizontal elastic pendulum

▶

A block (S) of mass m is fixed to a spring of stiffness k and negligible mass; the other end of the spring is fixed. (S) slides without friction on a horizontal support.

  • Axis (O,i) horizontal; O is the position of the centre of mass G at equilibrium (spring at its natural length).
  • Abscissa (elongation) of G: x=OG; velocity v=x′=dtdx​; acceleration a=v′=x′′.
  • Tension of the spring (Hooke): T=−kxi — always pointing back towards O ().
  • Reference for gravitational potential energy: the horizontal plane through G — so GPE=0 all the time.
At x = −3 cm the tension of the spring points in the negative sense.
When G passes through O, the resultant force on (S) is zero.

The differential equation — two roads, one result

▶

The official exams ask for it almost every year. Reveal each road line by line and say the next line before you click.

Road 1 · Energy (no friction)
ME=KE+EPE+GPE=21​mv2+21​kx2+0
No friction: ME is conserved, so dtd(ME)​=0.
21​m(2vv′)+21​k(2xx′)=0
with x′=v and v′=x′′: mvx′′+kxv=0
v is not always zero, so x′′+mk​x=0​
Road 2 · Newton's second law
Forces on (S): weight mg​, normal reaction N, tension T=−kxi.
ΣF=ma: mg​+N+T=ma
mg​+N=0 (horizontal motion), so −kxi=mx′′i
mx′′+kx=0
x′′+mk​x=0​ — the same equation ✔
Resultx′′+ω02​x=0 with ω0​=mk​​ (rad/s), the proper (natural) angular frequency. Any motion obeying this equation is called simple harmonic (sinusoidal).
Exam trapNever divide by v without saying “v is not always zero”. And write GPE = constant (horizontal motion), it is often worth a quarter point.

Energy: a perfect exchange

▶
EnergiesKE=21​mv2, EPE=21​kx2, ME=KE+EPE=21​kXm2​=21​mVm2​ = constant (no friction).
EPE and KE oscillate with half the period: Tenergy​=2T0​​. Versus x: EPE is a parabola, KE=21​k(Xm2​−x2) an inverted one.

Free damped oscillations

▶

With friction, the work of the friction force is negative: the mechanical energy decreases and turns into internal (thermal) energy. The amplitude decreases.

Live model · Turn up the friction and watch the mode change
ModesPseudo-periodic (weak friction): oscillations with decreasing amplitude, pseudo-period T slightly greater than T0​ (T≈T0​ when friction is weak). Aperiodic (strong friction): G returns to O without oscillating. The fastest return without oscillation is the critical case.
EnergyBetween two instants: ΔME=ME2​−ME1​=Wfriction​<0. At two extreme positions v=0: ΔME=21​k(X22​−X12​).
With a viscous friction f​=−hvdtd(ME)​= power of f​=−hv2: mvx′′+kxv=−hv2⇒x′′+mh​x′+mk​x=0​.

10

Driving (maintaining) the oscillations

▶

A pendulum clock, a child on a swing, a quartz watch: they keep the same amplitude for hours. A device gives the oscillator, at the right moments, exactly the energy lost by friction.

Live model · Switch on the maintaining device
Maintained oscillations“Driving the oscillations” means supplying the oscillator with the energy needed to compensate for its losses, so that its amplitude stays constant. The oscillations then have the period of the free oscillator (≈ T0​).
Average power supplied during Δt: Pav​=ΔtE​, where E=∣ΔME∣ lost during Δt without the device.
Official exam style (LS 2009)Released from 12.5 cm; after 10 oscillations the amplitude is 12 cm, k=80 N/m, T≈T0​=0.1π s. ΔME=21​(80)(0.122−0.1252)=−0.049 J. To maintain: Pav​=10×0.1π0.049​≈0.016 W.

▶

A small heavy ball hanging from a light string of length ℓ. For small oscillations, its proper period is T0​=2πgℓ​​ (given in the exams): independent of the mass, and of the amplitude if it is small.

Live model · Change ℓ, the angle — or the planet

13

Exam method: a collision starts the oscillation

▶

A classic two-chapter exercise: a bullet or a puck hits a block fixed to a spring. Two laws, in two stages.

Live model · Bullet embedded in a block (M = 0.99 kg, k = 100 N/m)
Two stages① The shock (very short): linear momentum is conserved, mv=(M+m)V0​ — not the kinetic energy (inelastic). ② The oscillation: ω0​=M+mk​​, launched from O with V0​: Xm​=ω0​V0​​, φ=0 (or π). Energy check: 21​(M+m)V02​=21​kXm2​.
