Part 1 · From intuition to Newton's second law

What does it take to stop something?

Open each step, play with the live models, then check yourself with Cards 1 and Practice 1. Words in open a short explanation.

1

Feel it: who is harder to stop?

▶

Pick two moving objects and compare. Stopping a body is hard when it is heavy or when it is fast — the quantity that measures both together is the linear momentum.

ThinkA bullet is light but very fast; a tanker is very slow but enormous. Which one has more momentum? Try it above.
2

The formula: a vector, not just a number

▶
DefinitionA particle of mass m moving with the velocity v has the linear momentum P=mv(unit: kg⋅m/s) P has the direction and sense of v; its magnitude is P=mv. In a frame (O,i,j​): Px​=mvx​, Py​=mvy​.
Live model · Drag the tip of the velocity
Watch the signA ball of 0.2 kg moving at 4 m/s towards the negative sense of the axis: v=−4i, so P=−0.8i (kg·m/s). The minus sign carries the direction.
3

Linear momentum of a system

▶
SystemThe linear momentum of a system is the vector sum of the momenta of its parts: Psys​=P1​+P2​+⋯=m1​v1​+m2​v2​+…
Live model · Drag the two arrow tips · Challenge: make Psys​=0
SurpriseTwo carts move towards each other: m1​=2 kg, v1​=3i; m2​=3 kg, v2​=−2i. Psys​=6i−6i=0: both move, yet the system has zero momentum.

5

Newton's second law

▶
LawIn a , the sum of the external forces acting on a system equals the time-derivative of its linear momentum: ΣFext​=dtdP​ One particle of constant mass: dtdP​=ma, so ΣFext​=ma. Internal forces cancel in pairs.
Live model · A constant force makes P grow linearly — slope = force

A ball is kicked; the contact lasts 0.02 s.

6

Isolated system: momentum is conserved

▶
DefinitionA system is isolated if no external force acts on it, or if the external forces cancel: ΣFext​=0. Then dtdP​=0: Psys​ is constant and G moves in a straight line at constant velocity (or stays at rest).

Bonus — principle of interactionFor two bodies forming an isolated system: dtdP1​​+dtdP2​​=0, so F2→1​=−F1→2​.
7

Parabolic motion: two components, two behaviours

▶

A ball is thrown with v0​ at the angle α; air resistance is neglected. The only force is the weight mg​=−mgj​.

Along xdtdPx​​=0⇒Px​=mv0​cosα=constant
Along ydtdPy​​=−mg⇒Py​=−mgt+mv0​sinα

Exercise 1 — Two pucks on an air table6 pts

Two pucks (A) and (B), of masses mA​=0.2 kg and mB​=0.3 kg, slide on a horizontal air table along the axis (O,i): xA​=0.5t−0.2 and xB​=−0.2t+0.6 (SI).

  1. 1.1Determine vA​ and vB​. In which sense does each puck move?
    Velocity is the derivative of the position: v=dtdx​.
    vA​=0.5i (positive sense); vB​=−0.2i (negative sense), in m/s.
  2. 1.2Calculate PA​, PB​ and the linear momentum P of the system (A, B).
    PA​=0.1i; PB​=−0.06i; P=0.04i (kg·m/s).
  3. 1.3Show that xG​=0.08t+0.28 (SI). Deduce vG​, then verify that P=(mA​+mB​)vG​.
    xG​=mA​+mB​mA​xA​+mB​xB​​.
    xG​=0.50.2(0.5t−0.2)+0.3(−0.2t+0.6)​=0.50.04t+0.14​=0.08t+0.28. vG​=0.08i; 0.5×0.08=0.04=P.
  4. 1.4Derive P with respect to time. What do you conclude about the external forces? Name them and explain why they cancel.
    dtdP​=0⇒ΣFext​=0. Weights and air-cushion reactions cancel (vertical); no friction: pseudo-isolated system.
  5. 1.5Specify the nature of the motion of G.
    vG​ constant: uniform rectilinear motion at 0.08 m/s.

Exercise 2 — A sled in Mzaar, then a volleyball smash7 pts

Part A. A sled carrying a child (M=40 kg) slides down a straight snowy slope inclined by α (sinα=0.10). The axis (O,i) is along the slope, downward. The snow exerts a friction force f​ of constant magnitude. A phone app gives P=(20t+40)i (SI).

  1. A.1Name the external forces acting on the system (sled + child).
    Weight Mg​, normal reaction N, friction f​.
  2. A.2Applying Newton's second law, determine ΣFext​. Deduce f.
    Project on i: only the weight component Mgsinα and −f remain.
    ΣFext​=dtdP​=20i (N). Mgsinα−f=20⇒f=40−20=20 N.
  3. A.3Determine the velocity at t=0 and the acceleration. Specify the nature of the motion.
    v=P/M=(0.5t+1)i: v0​=1 m/s; a=0.5i m/s2, constant, same sense as v: uniformly accelerated rectilinear motion.
  4. A.4Calculate ΔP between t=0 and t=4 s. Verify that ΔP=ΣFext​Δt. Why is this allowed here?
    ΔP=120i−40i=80i; ΣFΔt=20×4=80. Allowed because ΣFext​ is constant.

Part B. A volleyball (m=0.27 kg) arrives horizontally at 8 m/s and leaves in the opposite sense at 12 m/s. The contact lasts Δt=0.02 s. Take i in the sense of the final velocity.

  1. B.1Write Pi​ and Pf​; calculate ΔP. (A student writes ΔP=0.27×(12−8): find the mistake.)
    Pi​=−2.16i, Pf​=3.24i, ΔP=5.40i. The senses are opposite: Δv=12−(−8)=20 m/s.
  2. B.2Calculate the average force of the hands on the ball. Compare with the weight and conclude.
    Favg​=5.40/0.02=270 N along i. Weight =2.7 N: 100 times smaller, negligible during the shock.

Part 2 · Collisions and explosions

When bodies hit or break apart

During a very short interaction, momentum is the quantity you can trust.

8

Why momentum is conserved in a collision or an explosion

▶
Key ideaThe interaction lasts a very short time. The internal forces are huge; the external forces (weights, reactions, friction) are finite and act during a negligible time. The system is : Psys​(just before)=Psys​(just after)
Method① System = all the bodies that interact. ② Choose an axis. ③ Write P just before and just after. ④ Equate, then project (signs!).
TrapA ball bouncing on a wall: the wall (Earth) is external; the momentum of the ball alone is not conserved.

Explosion from rest

Explosion from rest0=m1​v1​+m2​v2​⇒v2​=−m2​m1​​v1​: opposite senses; the lighter part is faster. G stays at rest. The kinetic energy comes from internal (chemical or elastic) energy.
9

Elastic collision — the main case

▶
ElasticThe linear momentum and the kinetic energy of the system are conserved. Head-on, m2​ initially at rest: m1​v1​=m1​v1′​+m2​v2′​21​m1​v12​=21​m1​v1′2​+21​m2​v2′2​
Solving trickm1​(v1​−v1′​)=m2​v2′​ and m1​(v12​−v1′2​)=m2​v2′2​. Divide: v1​+v1′​=v2′​. Then v1′​=m1​+m2​m1​−m2​​v1​v2′​=m1​+m2​2m1​​v1​
CaseResult
m1​=m2​Exchange: v1′​=0, v2′​=v1​ (billiards, Newton's cradle)
m1​<m2​v1′​<0: (1) bounces back
m1​>m2​Both move forward
Live model · Collision lab — try each case of the table
“Show that the collision is elastic”(official exam 2021): calculate the kinetic energy of the system just before and just after, and show they are equal.
10

Inelastic collision — just know it

▶

Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not: part of it becomes internal energy (deformation, heat, sound). If the bodies stick together (perfectly inelastic, “soft” collision) they share a common velocity V=m1​+m2​m1​v1​+m2​v2​​. Try “Stick together” in the lab above.

Exercise 1 — Fireworks and a hunting rifle6 pts

Part A. A firework shell (M=1.2 kg) is launched vertically. At the top of its path (velocity zero) it explodes into two fragments: (1) of mass m1​=0.4 kg, thrown horizontally with v1​=30i (m/s), and (2) of mass m2​.

  1. A.1Why is the shell a pseudo-isolated system during the explosion, although subject to its weight?
    The explosion is very short; the internal forces are much larger than the weight, whose effect during that time is negligible.
  2. A.2What is P just before the explosion? Determine v2​.
    P=0. m2​=0.8 kg: 0.4×30i+0.8v2​=0⇒v2​=−15i (m/s).
  3. A.3Calculate the kinetic energy gained. Where does it come from?
    21​(0.4)(30)2+21​(0.8)(15)2=180+90=270 J, from the chemical (internal) energy of the powder.
  4. A.4What is the velocity of G just after the explosion?
    vG​=P/M=0.

Part B. A hunting rifle (4.0 kg) fires a 10 g bullet at 400 m/s from rest.

  1. B.1Calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle held loosely.
    0.01×400+4.0v=0⇒v=−1.0 m/s.
  2. B.2The hunter (70 kg) holds it firmly against his shoulder. New recoil velocity? Why is it safer?
    v=−4/74≈−0.05 m/s: the recoiling mass is much larger, so the recoil is much slower and softer.

Exercise 2 — Elastic collision on an air track8 pts

Glider (S1​): m1​=0.2 kg; glider (S2​): m2​=0.3 kg, at rest. Spring: k=80 N/m, compressed by d=5 cm and released; (S1​) leaves the spring before hitting (S2​). No friction.
  1. 2.1Show that (S1​) leaves the spring with v1​=1.0 m/s.
    21​kd2=21​m1​v12​: 0.1 J=0.1v12​⇒v1​=1.0 m/s.
  2. 2.2Why is the momentum of (S1​,S2​) conserved during the collision?
    Weights and reactions cancel, no friction, short collision: pseudo-isolated system.
  3. 2.3The collision is elastic. Write the two conservation relations in terms of v1′​ and v2′​.
    0.2(1)=0.2v1′​+0.3v2′​; 21​(0.2)(1)2=21​(0.2)v1′2​+21​(0.3)v2′2​.
  4. 2.4Deduce v1​+v1′​=v2′​, then calculate v1′​ and v2′​. Interpret the sign of v1′​.
    Write 0.2(1−v1′​)=0.3v2′​ and 0.2(1−v1′2​)=0.3v2′2​, then divide.

    vG​=0.2/0.5=0.4 m/s before and after: the collision does not change the motion of G.

    Equal masses: exchange, v1′​=0, v2′​=1.0 m/s.

    V=0.2/0.5=0.4 m/s; KE′=0.04 J; lost 0.06 J (60 %).
